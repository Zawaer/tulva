## What it shows
- Both IBM and IQM hardware run the joint-flood amplitude-estimation circuit; raw readings are 2–3× the exact value (readout errors and gate noise), and the two-circuit calibration removes most of that bias.
- Shallow circuits survive (3 qubits, ≤ ~50 two-qubit gates); the deeper 4-qubit amplified circuits lose most of their signal, especially on IBM. That is the measured limit of today's hardware.
- Classical Monte Carlo at the same budget is still more precise. No quantum advantage is claimed.
- 'Best qubits' on IBM uses today's calibration data; IQM publishes none through this API, so there the copies are only placed on well-separated qubits. The first IQM run had 1 copy (small sample, ±0.05 pp shot noise).
- VTT Q50 (three repeats, section below) is not there yet: raw readings ~12× exact, amplified circuits keep little signal, and the calibrated estimate is not reproducible between repeats.
- The independence line (0.040 %) is what P(both) would be if the two areas flooded independently: every run, even raw, is far above it, i.e. the hardware reproduces the co-flooding.
