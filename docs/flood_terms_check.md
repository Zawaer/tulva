# Finnish home insurance: "exceptional flood" definitions, checked against primary sources

Checked 2026-10-09 against public terms PDFs and web pages. Every quote below comes from the insurer's own document, which was downloaded and searched for "50 vuodessa" / "esiintymistodennäköisyys". Third-party summaries were not used as evidence.

## Bottom line

- **All 7 insurers use the same threshold.** OP Pohjola, If, LähiTapiola, Fennia, Turva, Pohjantähti and POP Vakuutus all define an exceptional river, lake or sea flood as a water level with *esiintymistodennäköisyys kerran 50 vuodessa tai harvemmin* (1-in-50 years or rarer). None uses 1-in-20, and none drops the return period.
- **The wording is shared almost word for word.** The same sentence appears everywhere: *"Poikkeuksellisena vedenpinnan tai merenpinnan nousuna pidetään vedenkorkeutta, jonka esiintymistodennäköisyys on kerran 50 vuodessa tai harvemmin. Poikkeuksellista ei ole pysyvästä keskivedenpinnan noususta tai vedenpinnan tason normaalista vaihtelusta tai aallokosta johtuva tulva."* This is a de facto market standard, but it is not a published Finanssiala standard clause (see below).
- **The 1-in-50 test applies to river/lake floods (vesistötulva) and sea floods (merivesitulva).** Every insurer lists snowmelt (*lumen sulaminen*) and ice or frazil-ice jams (*jää- tai hyydepato*) as causes of a river/lake flood. That is the spring-flood case.
- **Heavy rain (rankkasadetulva) uses a different test: rainfall intensity, not a return period.** If, Fennia and Pohjantähti use 30 mm/h or 75 mm/day. LähiTapiola and Turva use 30 mm/h or 70 mm/day. OP's and POP's terms say only "poikkeuksellinen rankkasade" and give no mm threshold.
- **Exception in OP and POP: storm-wind water rise.** Their wording counts a water rise caused by storm wind as exceptional *in addition to* (*sekä* / *ja*) a 1-in-50 water level. So a wind-driven rise can qualify without meeting the 50-year test. The other five list storm wind only as a *cause* in the flood definition and still apply the 50-year test.
- **Ordinary spring floods are explicitly not covered.** Turva's 2026 home insurance guide lists *"Tavanomainen kevättulva aiheuttaa rakennukselle vahinkoa"* (an ordinary spring flood damages the building) as **"Ei korvaa"** (not covered) at all three cover levels.
- **Cover depends on the product tier.** Flood cover is not in every tier everywhere:
  - Turva: Laaja and Perus cover it; Suppea does not.
  - Pohjantähti: the older KO100 terms excluded river/lake floods.
  - POP: flood cover is always included.

## Per insurer

### OP Pohjola (Pohjola Vakuutus)
- **Source:** *Kodin ja tavaroiden vakuutukset, Mittaturvan vakuutusehdot 1.1.2025* (form code 330215f 1.25). This is the version currently served by op.fi: https://www.op.fi/tac?did=Heoma0000001018&cs=ecbc6782c6221570f0970c519f65ace53aef8defb4395c8a5164f39f9d5b06ef
- **Quote:** "Poikkeuksellisella vesistön tai meren vedenpinnan nousulla tarkoitetaan myrskytuulen aiheuttamaa vedenpinnan nousua sekä vedenkorkeutta, jonka esiintymistodennäköisyys on kerran 50 vuodessa tai harvemmin."
- **English:** "An exceptional rise of a water body or the sea means a water rise caused by storm wind, as well as a water level whose probability of occurrence is once in 50 years or less often."
- **Return period:** 1-in-50, plus any rise caused by storm wind.
- **Flood types:**
  - River/lake/pond/brook (*vesistötulva*): caused by storm wind, exceptional rain, snowmelt, or ice/frazil-ice jams.
  - Sea flood: caused by storm wind, air-pressure changes, or flows in the Danish straits.
  - Heavy rain: listed separately as "rankkasateesta", with no mm threshold in this document.
  - All three sit under *Palo- ja luonnonilmiöturva*.

### If
- **Source:** *Kodin Omaisuusvakuutusehdot, Ehto OMA 200.27, voimassa 1.1.2026 alkaen*: https://www.if.fi/globalassets/fi/pdf/ehdot/kotivakuutusehdot.pdf
- **Quote:** "Poikkeuksellisena vedenpinnan tai merenpinnan nousuna pidetään vedenkorkeutta, jonka esiintymistodennäköisyys on kerran 50 vuodessa tai harvemmin."
- **English:** "An exceptional rise of water or sea level means a water level whose probability of occurrence is once in 50 years or less often."
- **Return period:** 1-in-50.
- **Flood types:**
  - River/lake (*vesistötulva*, defined as "joessa tai järvessä", so streams and ditches are not named): caused by exceptional rain, snowmelt, or ice/frazil-ice jams.
  - Sea flood.
  - Heavy rain: 30 mm/h or 75 mm/day.
  - Covered under *Luonnonilmiöturva* (natural-hazard cover). Yard damage is excluded for river and sea floods.
- **Also confirmed in:** If's *Kotivakuutusopas, voimassa 1.1.2026*: https://www.if.fi/globalassets/fi/pdf/opas/kotivakuutusopas.pdf

### LähiTapiola
- **Source:** *Kotivakuutus, Vakuutusehdot, voimassa 1.1.2026 alkaen*, section 5.1.3 "Poikkeuksellinen tulva": https://public.egate.fi/lahitapiola/lahitapiola/fi/tiedostot/226619/ (the 1.1.2024 version has identical wording: https://core-public.editaprima.fi/lahitapiola/download/4671/10/L-EH-04100-fi_JNyZhh3nv1)
- **Quote:** "Poikkeuksellisena vedenpinnan tai merenpinnan nousuna pidetään vedenkorkeutta, jonka esiintymistodennäköisyys on kerran 50 vuodessa tai harvemmin."
- **English:** Same as If: a water level with a probability of once in 50 years or less often.
- **Return period:** 1-in-50.
- **Flood types:**
  - River/lake/brook: caused by exceptional rain, snowmelt, or ice jams.
  - Sea flood: storm wind is defined as average wind above 15 m/s.
  - Heavy rain: 30 mm/h or 70 mm/day.
  - Ice movement caused by a river or sea flood is also covered.
- **Other products with the same 50-year definition:**
  - LähiTapiola's business product *Poikkeuksellisten tulvien vakuutus ES4*, valid from 1.8.2025: https://core-public.editaprima.fi/lahitapiola/download/7204/10
  - LähiTapiola's *Kiinteistövakuutus* (property insurance), 1.1.2026.

### Fennia
- **Source:** *Fenniaturvan vakuutusehdot F20–F100, voimassa 1.1.2026 alkaen*, sections F21 Laaja kotivakuutus and F22 Peruskotivakuutus (F22.1.11.1), on an HTML page: https://www.fennia.fi/fenniaturvan-vakuutusehdot-1
- **Quote:** "Poikkeuksellisena veden pinnan tai meren pinnan nousuna pidetään veden korkeutta, jonka esiintymistodennäköisyys on kerran 50 vuodessa tai harvemmin."
- **English:** Same meaning as above.
- **Return period:** 1-in-50.
- **Flood types:**
  - River/lake/ditch/brook: caused by storm wind, exceptional rain, snowmelt, or ice jams.
  - Sea flood.
  - Heavy rain: 30 mm/h or 75 mm/day.
  - Combinations of flood types, and ice movement.
  - Applies to both the Laaja and Perus home insurance tiers.
- **Note:** The search engines surface an older Fennia business flood policy (YT111 Tulvavakuutus, 1.11.2013, https://cs.fennia.fi/lomakepalvelu/tiedostot/VLE2035). It has the same 50-year test, but it is not the current home product.

- **English:** Same meaning as above.
- **Return period:** 1-in-50.
- **Flood types:**
  - River/lake/brook: caused by exceptional rain, snowmelt, or ice jams.
  - Sea flood: storm wind above 15 m/s.
  - Heavy rain: 30 mm/h or 70 mm/day.
- **Supporting guide:** *Koti turvassa – Kodin vakuutusopas 1.1.2026*: https://core-public.editaprima.fi/turva/download/206/2

### POP Vakuutus
- **Source:** *POP Vakuutus – Kotivakuutusehdot, voimassa 1.11.2021 alkaen*, section 4.2 Tulvaturva. This is the version linked from popvakuutus.fi today: https://www.popvakuutus.fi/api/terms/type/terms/classifier/HOM/locale/fi
- **Quote:** "Poikkeuksellisella vesistön tai meren vedenpinnan nousulla tarkoitetaan myrskytuulen aiheuttamaa vedenpinnan nousua ja vedenkorkeutta, jonka esiintymistodennäköisyys on kerran 50 vuodessa tai harvemmin."
- **English:** "An exceptional rise of a water body or the sea means a water rise caused by storm wind and a water level whose probability of occurrence is once in 50 years or less often."

- **Flood types:**
  - River/lake/ditch/brook: caused by storm wind, exceptional rain, snowmelt, or ice jams.
  - Sea flood.
  - Heavy rain: "poikkeuksellinen rankkasade", with no mm threshold in the terms.
  - Flood cover is always included. Yard damage is excluded.

## Finanssiala (Finance Finland) and other industry sources

- **No common industry definition or recommended clause was found.** Finanssiala's report *Vahinkovakuuttaminen muuttuvassa ilmastossa* (21.4.2021) says: "Vakuutusyhtiöt määrittelevät poikkeuksellisuuden eri tavoin. Sääilmiö on poikkeuksellinen, kun se esiintyy kerran 50 vuodessa tai harvemmin." In English: "Insurers define exceptionality in different ways. A weather event is exceptional when it occurs once in 50 years or less often." Source: https://www.finanssiala.fi/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Vahinkovakuuttaminen-muuttuvassa-ilmastossa-raportti-21042021.pdf
- **That describes market practice; it is not a binding standard.** The identical wording across insurers suggests a shared origin, probably from when the state flood-compensation scheme ended on 1.1.2014. I could not verify that origin.
- **FINE (the consumer insurance complaints board) applies the 1-in-50 test strictly**, usually relying on the environmental authorities' (SYKE / ELY) estimate of the return period. For example, FINE-045014: https://www.fine.fi/ota-yhteytta/ratkaisutietokanta/ratkaisu/fine-045014.html (summary only; the full case was not read).
- **Broker case on the same test:** Howden describes a housing-company property policy where a river-flood claim was denied because SYKE assessed the level as recurring more often than once in 20 years. Here "1-in-20" was the *assessed* frequency used to deny the claim, not a threshold in any policy: https://www.howdengroup.com/fi-fi/tulvavedet-vyoryivat-taloyhtion-kellariin-korvasiko-vakuutus-mitaan

## Unverified / caveats

- **OP:** I checked the Mittaturva terms dated 1.1.2025, which op.fi currently serves. I could not confirm whether a 1.1.2026 version exists. I also did not check whether OP defines a mm threshold for heavy rain in a separate document.
- **POP:** The terms are dated 1.11.2021 and are what popvakuutus.fi links to now. I did not verify whether POP has newer terms that are not yet published.
- **Fennia:** I verified the HTML terms page only (dated 1.1.2026), not a PDF.
- **Insurers not checked:** Smaller ones such as Aktia (sells If/LähiTapiola products), Säästöpankki (sells Fennia/LähiTapiola) and Alandia are not covered here.
- **Policy-level variation:** Individual policies can carry special terms (*erityisehdot*). Lower tiers may exclude flood (Turva Suppea, Pohjantähti KO100 legacy).

## Safe pitch wording

You can safely say: "All major Finnish home insurers we checked (OP Pohjola, If, LähiTapiola, Fennia, Turva, Pohjantähti, POP), roughly the whole consumer market, only cover river, lake and sea floods, including snowmelt/ice-jam spring floods, when the water level reaches a 1-in-50-year or rarer event; ordinary spring floods are explicitly not covered." Avoid calling it an official industry standard or a Finanssiala rule, and note that heavy-rain flooding is judged by rainfall intensity (30 mm/h or 70–75 mm/day), not by the 50-year return period.
