    2 · March snow outlook

    Chance that ≥2 areas in a cluster pass their 1-in-50 level this spring

    In plain words: how likely is it that two or more rivers in the same region have a big flood this spring? A gauge is a measuring station on a river that records how much water flows past. The 1-in-50 level is a flow so high it happens only once every 50 years on average. Move the slider to see how this winter's snow changes the odds.

    less snowmore snow

    Probabilities: fitted Gaussian-copula factor model, conditioned on the cluster's 1 March SWE index using its in-sample snow skill; computed by quadrature (exact for the model; Monte Carlo agrees, hover the labels). High / low snow = top / bottom 15 % of 1 March SWE. Technical index price = probability × €10M × (1 + risk load); market price = 2.5–3.5× the expected payout (cat-bond / flood-ILS multiples), both from out/pricing.json. The snow skill rests on 65 years and is uncertain.

    3 · The product

    How it works

    From river data to a quantum answer

    One real example, start to finish: will Kittilä and Ivalo both pass their 1-in-50 flood level in the same spring? Pick the kind of March and follow the numbers.

    One example, step by step

    This March:
    1

    River records go in

    The highest river flow of every spring since 1961, from SYKE gauges. The dashed line is the 1-in-50 level, a flood that comes once in 50 years. Red: 2005, when both rivers passed it together.

    2

    Snow on 1 March goes in

    How much water is stored in the snow two months before the melt. More snow means more meltwater. 2005 was one of the snowiest Marches.

    3

    The model boils 65 years down to a few numbers

    Rivers in the north flood together because they share the same wet spring. The snow changes how likely a wet spring is.

    4

    The numbers become a quantum circuit

    A circuit is a recipe of steps for the qubits. One qubit decides "wet spring?", and it steers the two river qubits, so they tend to flood together, just like the real rivers.

    5

    The quantum computer answers in shots

    One shot = run the recipe once and read every qubit: 1 = flood, 0 = no flood. Each square below is one shot on the real IQM Emerald quantum computer, like one simulated spring.

    6

    Count, correct, compare

    Count the red squares. Noise makes the chip see some fake floods, so we also run test circuits with known answers and use them to correct. Then we check against the exact answer.

    ShotOne run of the circuit, read once. Like rolling the dice once.
    NoiseTiny errors in the hardware. More steps, more errors.
    Test circuitSame recipe with made-up numbers whose answer we know, to measure the noise.

    4 · Under the hood

    The joint flood risk, run on real quantum computers

    Classical exact values are the reference. Hardware results from IBM and IQM chips are checked against them, and against classical Monte Carlo with the same budget. We make no claim of quantum advantage.
