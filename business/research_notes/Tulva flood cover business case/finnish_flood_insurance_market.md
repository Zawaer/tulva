# Finnish River/Spring Flood Insurance Market and Flood Loss History

Research date: 2026-10-08. Note: several primary sources (Finance Finland PDFs, SYKE/ELY reports) were text-extracted locally from the PDFs at the URLs cited. Searches were done in English and Finnish. Where a figure only came from a search-engine summary of a page and could not be read in full, this is flagged.

## 1. How flood cover works in Finland since the 2014 reform

### Takeaway
On 1 Jan 2014 the state flood-damage compensation act (284/1983) was allowed to lapse. Since then, flood damage to buildings and contents is covered only through voluntary private home and property insurance ("tulvaturva"). Nearly all insurers bundled flood cover into home and property policies. Cover is triggered only by an *exceptional* flood: for river (vesistö) floods, one with a statistical return period of 1-in-50 years or rarer, as certified case by case by SYKE/Tulvakeskus. Crop and agricultural flood losses stayed with a state scheme until 2016, then moved to insurance. **This 1-in-50 trigger in the policy wording is the same threshold Tulva proposes to use, which is directly relevant to product design.**

### Cited Findings
- The act on compensation for damage caused by exceptional floods (Laki poikkeuksellisten tulvien aiheuttamien vahinkojen korvaamisesta, 284/1983) lapsed on 1 Jan 2014. Some agricultural flood provisions were moved into the crop damage act (1214/2000). That act was itself to be repealed from 1 Jan 2017, but 2014–2015 damage was still compensated under it — [HE 143/2013 (edilex)](https://www.edilex.fi/he/20130143); [Parliament committee report MmVM 19/2013](https://www.riksdagen.fi/pdf/MmVM+19/2013)
- Rationale in the government bill: the owner carries primary responsibility for their own property and for preparing against damage to it. After the reform the state no longer compensates flood damage to buildings or contents — [Kuopio city document citing the reform](https://publish.kuopio.fi/kokous/2018508455-7.PDF); [HE 143/2013](https://www.edilex.fi/he/20130143)
- Old state system parameters (crop/flood scheme, per HE 143/2013):
  - Farmer deductible was 30% of the farm's normal crop value, and the state paid 90% of losses above that.
  - The bill proposed a transitional flood-compensation cap of 80% of the damage, net of other compensation, with payments under €100 excluded.
  - Annual budget appropriation was €3.4M.
  - State compensation averaged €4.3M/yr in the 2000s, ranging €0.30M–€19.5M/yr (2004: €19.5M; 2012: €9.24M).
  - Source: [HE 143/2013](https://www.edilex.fi/he/20130143)
- During the bill consultation, Finance Finland said two years was very short to develop new products, and that an insurance-based system would need financial support to encourage take-up — [HE 143/2013](https://www.edilex.fi/he/20130143)
- Finance Finland's stated rationale: compensating damage is not a core state function but is core business for insurers, and claims handling would become much faster. The scope of covered flood damage was also widened beyond river/lake floods to include heavy-rain (stormwater) floods and sea-level rise — [Verkkouutiset, "Tulvavahinkojen korvaaminen nopeutuu"](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen-nopeutuu-12997/); [Yle: "Tulvavahinkojen korvaaminen valtiolta vakuutusyhtiöille"](https://yle.fi/a/3-7001985)
- "Almost all insurers" attached flood cover to home and property insurance. Home and property insurance is voluntary, and without it the owner bears flood costs in full. Cover for outdoor property varies by insurer, and policy terms differ between companies — [Taloustaito](https://www.taloustaito.fi/koti/rankkasateet-voivat-voimistua--korvaako-vakuutus/) (quoting Finance Finland)
- The state does not compensate flood damage ("Valtio ei korvaa tulvista koituvia vahinkoja"). Home insurance pays only if the flood is *exceptional* as defined in the terms, generally a flood that statistically occurs once in 50 years or less often. Insurers can request a recurrence statement from Tulvakeskus (the Flood Centre), and exceptionality can only be determined after the event — [vesi.fi: Tulvavahinkojen korvaaminen (28 Jun 2021)](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/)
- SYKE issues an exceptionality statement for each loss, which can take weeks, and no compensation is paid before it is issued. Damage from driving a car into floodwater is generally not covered, while a parked car can be covered (under comprehensive motor insurance) — [Yle 74-20032881](https://yle.fi/a/74-20032881) / [Yle 74-20235029](https://yle.fi/a/74-20235029) (summarised from a search result; the article was not read in full)
- Policies may require certain protective measures to be taken before a flood — [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/)
- Crop damage compensation moved from the state system to an insurance-based system in 2016 — [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/)
- Business property insurance ("esinevakuutus") is modular: fire, natural phenomena (luonnonilmiö), property crime, leakage, breakage and frozen-goods covers — [LähiTapiola document via search](https://core-public.editaprima.fi/lahitapiola/download/6691/10/V-OH-00153-fi_qCmDt5seib)

### Inferences
- Because cover is binary (exceptional = 1-in-50 or rarer, otherwise nothing), there is a structural protection gap for frequent (1-in-10 to 1-in-49) spring floods. These recur in Lapland and Ostrobothnia but are not insured. Insurers' losses are concentrated in the tail years.
- The insurer-side trigger already relies on a SYKE return-period statement. A parametric index on gauge discharge versus the 1-in-50 level aligns closely with insurers' own policy wording, which keeps basis risk low for the insurer as buyer.

### Gaps
- No standard published deductible for flood under home insurance was found. Deductibles are set per policy and insurer (typically the general home-insurance deductible); exact figures were not verified.
- Insurer-specific exclusions (e.g. groundwater, ice-jam, buildings below recommended lowest building elevation) were not verified from current policy terms.
- Home-insurance take-up rate and the share of policies that include flood cover: no reliable 2024–2026 figure found.

## 2. Insurers, market shares and premium volumes

### Takeaway
Finnish non-life insurance is a highly concentrated €5.6bn (2024) market. OP/Pohjola (32.6%), LocalTapiola (28.3%), If P&C (21.9%) and Fennia (8.4%) write more than 91% of it. "Fire and other damage to property", the class that contains home and property flood cover, is the largest non-life class at €1.28bn GWP in 2024.

### Cited Findings
All from [Finance Finland, "Finnish insurance in 2024" (22 Apr 2025)](https://www.finanssiala.fi/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/finnish-insurance-in-2024.pdf); Finnish version: [Vakuutusvuosi 2024](https://www.finanssiala.fi/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/fa_vakuutusvuosi_2024_liitteineen.pdf).

**Market totals and results, 2024**
- Total Finnish insurance premiums written in 2024 were €29.8bn (+5.2%).
- Non-life premiums written were €5.551bn (+3.2%; 2023: €5.380bn). Domestic direct non-life premiums were €5.315bn (2023: €5.155bn).
- Non-life combined ratio was 96.7%, loss ratio 75.5% and expense ratio 21.2%.
- Claims paid under direct non-life insurance rose 9.9%.
- "Fire and other damage to property" (business plus personal property, which includes home insurance):
  - GWP was €1,277M in 2024 (+4.3%; 2023: €1,225M).
  - Loss ratio was 74.5%, impaired by several major losses.
  - Claims paid were €874M (+12.1%).
- Accepted reinsurance premiums were €208M (+3.3%), with a loss ratio of 42.0%.
- There were 46 licensed Finnish insurers at end-2024, of which 34 are non-life/reinsurance.

### Inferences
- The addressable cedant pool for Tulva is essentially four groups (OP/Pohjola, LocalTapiola, If, Fennia), plus small mutuals (Turva, Pohjantähti, Alandia, Ålands Ömsesidiga).
- LocalTapiola is a federation of regional mutuals (e.g. LähiTapiola Lappi, Länsi-Suomi, Pohjanmaa). Those regional mutuals plus Pohjantähti and Turva have concentrated regional exposure and thinner capital, and Fennia, Turva and Pohjantähti all had combined ratios above 100% in 2024. These are plausible early buyers for a regional-cluster parametric cover.
- An order-of-magnitude scale check: if flood/nat-cat loading were even 1–3% of the €1.28bn property class, that implies roughly €13–38M/yr of nat-cat premium. A €10M parametric limit is material relative to that.

### Gaps
- No Finnish home-insurance-only (kotivakuutus) premium total was found. Finance Finland reports the combined "fire and other damage to property" class only.
- No flood-specific premium or loss statistics by insurer.

## 3. Reinsurance of nat-cat (flood/storm) by Finnish insurers

### Takeaway
No public detail on Finnish insurers' cat reinsurance programmes (retentions, limits, reinsurer panels) was found in the time available. Finance Finland reports only aggregate reinsurance. Finnish nat-cat exposure is generally dominated by windstorm and forest/property storm losses rather than flood.

### Cited Findings
- Accepted reinsurance written by Finnish non-life insurers was €208M in 2024, with a 42.0% loss ratio. Claims paid on reinsurance fell 48.6% — [Finance Finland 2024](https://www.finanssiala.fi/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/finnish-insurance-in-2024.pdf)
- Industry commentary: climate change is raising reinsurance claims and reinsurance premiums globally. Storm damage is expected to grow, and building near waterways continues despite more frequent floods — [Verkkouutiset, "Ilmastonmuutos nostaa vakuutusmaksuja"](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/ilmastonmuutos-nostaa-vakuutusmaksuja/)
- In Sept 2024, FIN-FSA reported that non-life sector Solvency II technical provisions grew 3.6% due to unfavourable claims development, while solvency remained strong — (via search summary of a FIN-FSA release; not read directly)
- Pohjola Insurance's financial strength ratings (Moody's, S&P) are listed on OP's site, but no reinsurance programme detail is given — [OP: Pohjola Vakuutus ratings](https://op.fi/fi/op-ryhma/velkasijoittajat/luottoluokitukset/pohjola-vakuutus-oy)

### Inferences
- Detailed cat programme data would likely sit in each insurer's SFCR (Solvency and Financial Condition Report), in its "reinsurance / risk mitigation" sections. Typically only qualitative statements are disclosed. Practical next step: read the 2024/2025 SFCRs of Pohjola, LocalTapiola, Fennia, Turva and Pohjantähti, and If's Sampo group disclosures.
- If P&C is part of the Sampo group, which runs a Nordic group-level reinsurance programme, so Finnish flood risk is pooled with Swedish, Norwegian and Danish risk. Domestic mutuals likely buy standalone cat excess-of-loss. *This is an inference and is not verified.*

### Gaps
- Retention, limit, and peril scope (storm vs flood) of any Finnish insurer's cat XL programme were not found.
- No public evidence was found of a Finnish flood cat bond or parametric deal.

## 4. Historical spring/river flood events and losses

### Takeaway
Finnish flood losses are small in absolute terms. The largest river or spring events cost roughly €5–20M in building damage. The worst historic single events are the 2007 Pori cloudburst (more than €20M total, about €10M insured) and the 2012–2013 Ostrobothnia floods (about €20M total). Insurers paid only about €5M of flood claims in total across 2010–2015, and there has been no systematic industry flood-claims statistic since 2015. Modelled tail scenarios are far larger (hundreds of €M), concentrated in Pori (Kokemäenjoki), Rovaniemi (Kemijoki) and Saimaa.

### Cited Findings

**Event history**
- **Spring 1966, southern Finland:**
  - Snow water equivalent (SWE) in early April was widely 200–250 mm, roughly double normal.
  - Return periods exceeded 100 years in several basins (Gumbel estimate).
  - Probable cost was "of the order of 10 million euros".
  - Source: [Helminen (FMI), Munich workshop paper](https://sciencepolicy.colorado.edu/research_areas/sparc/research/projects/extreme_events/munich_workshop/helminen.pdf)
- **1981, Ivalojoki:** building damage of nearly €270,000, plus €50,000 in protection, repair and rescue costs — [Inari master-plan annex on flooding (via search summary)](https://www.inari.fi/media/tek.-os.-kaavoitus/yleiskaavat/ivalon-alueen-yk-osa-alueet-123/liite6_tulva.pdf)
- **Summer 2004, heavy rain in southern and central Finland** (Riihimäki, Vöyri, Oravainen): about €8M in house damage, excluding harvest, business and public losses. This is a rain flood rather than a spring flood — [Helminen](https://sciencepolicy.colorado.edu/research_areas/sparc/research/projects/extreme_events/munich_workshop/helminen.pdf). State compensation peaked at €19.5M in 2004 — [HE 143/2013](https://www.edilex.fi/he/20130143)
- **Spring 2005, Lapland (Ivalo, Kittilä):**
  - SWE was above 180 mm in NW Lapland and the upper Kemijoki/Ivalojoki basins, about double normal.
  - Melt ran at up to 20 mm/day.
  - The Ounasjoki and Ivalojoki reached record levels, the highest in 50 years, with a return period of about 1-in-50.
  - The Ounasjoki rose 6 m at Kittilä, 88 cm above any previously measured level.
  - House damage was about €5M, excluding harvest, business and public losses — [Helminen](https://sciencepolicy.colorado.edu/research_areas/sparc/research/projects/extreme_events/munich_workshop/helminen.pdf)
  - **Conflicting figure:** Yle reported the 2005 floods caused about €17M of damage. The definition is likely broader (total economic, including public) — [Yle (search-result attribution; exact article URL uncertain, likely yle.fi/a/3-5225542)](https://yle.fi/a/3-5225542)
  - Flood barriers built after 2005 later kept central Ivalo dry — [Nunatsiaq News](https://nunatsiaq.com/stories/article/floods_wreak_havoc_in_finland_and_alaska/)
  - Kittilä flood protection: [Nordregio](https://archive.nordregio.org/en/Metameny/About-Nordregio/Journal-of-Nordregio/2008/Journal-of-Nordregio-no-1-1008/Flood-protection-in-Kittila-Finland/index.html)
- **Pori, August 2007 cloudburst** (urban/stormwater, not river): damage above €20M, of which insurers paid about €10M — [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/). This was pre-reform, so insured stormwater cover existed separately from the state river-flood scheme.
- **2012 and 2013 floods (Ostrobothnia):** the state paid over €4M, and total damage was estimated at about €20M — [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/). A 2012 supplementary budget motion estimated €10–15M in claims for residential buildings and contents, and proposed a €25M addition — [Parliament LTA 97/2012](https://www.riksdagen.fi/pdf/LTA+97/2012)
- **2010–2015 insured flood claims:** insurers paid more than €5M in flood damage compensation in total — [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/)
- **After 2015:** Finance Finland said there has been no statistical tracking of flood compensation amounts — [Taloustaito (2021)](https://www.taloustaito.fi/koti/rankkasateet-voivat-voimistua--korvaako-vakuutus/). SYKE collected insurer flood-claims data up to 2015 and "aims to continue". A Power BI flood damage statistics report covers 1995–2015 — [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/)
- **Spring 2018, Lapland:** forecasts called it the worst in decades (SYKE: worst in 20 years). Lapland ELY expected the Kittilä peak about 70 cm above the damage threshold, and Copernicus EMS was activated — [RCI / Eye on the Arctic](https://www.rcinet.ca/eye-on-the-arctic/2018/05/14/finland-lapland-saimaa-flooding-weather/); [Barents Observer](https://thebarentsobserver.com/en/ecology/2018/05/lapland-braces-worst-spring-floods-decades); [Copernicus EMS, 17 May 2018](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/news/the-copernicus-emergency-management-service-monitors-floods-in-finland/). **No € loss total was found.**
- **Spring 2020, Lapland:** ELY warned that water would rise to the damage limit in Rovaniemi and Kittilä — [ELY-keskus 2020](https://ely-keskus.fi/uutiset-2020/-/asset_publisher/OvA3wfzIQ4AO/content/flood-water-will-rise-to-the-damage-limit-in-rovaniemi-and-kittila-in-the-next-few-days-lapland-). No € loss figure was found.
- **Spring 2024:**
  - SYKE warned the worst situation was in Ostrobothnia and South Ostrobothnia, where floods were forecast to approach 2012/2013 levels. A STT release said the west coast was flooding and Lapland faced a difficult flood season — [STT/SYKE release](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70135434/lansirannikolla-tulvii-myos-lappiin-ennakoidaan-kevaan-edetessa-vaikeaa-tulvakauttapublisherId=69819243&lang=fi)
  - Ivalojoki in May 2024 was forecast as "larger than average but clearly smaller than the 2005 major flood", staying clearly below the damage limit in central Ivalo. Roads were cut locally and rescue services evacuated four people by boat — [Lapland ELY/STT release](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70212999/lapin-jokien-tulvahuiput-odotettavissa-viikonvaihteessa-tai-ensi-viikon-aikana?publisherId=69817880&lang=fi); [Yle 74-20089415](https://yle.fi/a/74-20089415)
  - Inari and Lapland ELY held a public meeting on the Ivalojoki spring flood on 7 May 2024 — [Inari](https://www.inari.fi/media/tiedostot-2024/hallinto-ja-yritys-ja-khittamispalvelut/ohjelma_ivalon_yleisotilaisuus_07052024.pdf)
  - **No € loss total was found for 2024.**
  - An exceptional *autumn* flood hit western Finland in November 2024, including Lapväärtti — [SYKE/STT](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70709427/poikkeuksellinen-syystulva-lansi-suomessa?lang=fi); [Pohjalaismaakunnat ELY release](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70780902/pohjalaismaakuntien-alueelle-nimettiin-nelja-merkittavaa-tulvariskialuetta-pohjalaismaakunnat?lang=fi)
  - LähiTapiola Länsi-Suomi's 2024 report says paid claims rose notably because of late-year storms and flood — [LähiTapiola Länsi-Suomi report](https://core-public.editaprima.fi/lahitapiola/download/7167/10) (via search summary)
- **Spring 2025:**
  - Northern Lapland had above-average snow and southern Lapland below-average.
  - By 13 May, forecasts showed low peaks spread out over time by cool weather. Ivalojoki and Simojoki had separate peaks.
  - No damaging flood was reported — [Lapland flood meeting notes 29 Apr 2025](https://www.ely-keskus.fi/documents/d/ely-keskus/kokouskooste_tulvakokous_lappi_3_2025_29042025); [13 May 2025](https://www.ely-keskus.fi/documents/d/ely-keskus/kokouskooste_tulvakokous_lappi_5_2025_13052025)
- **Spring 2026:**
  - Spring floods were forecast small or at most ordinary — [Yle 74-20213705](https://yle.fi/a/74-20213705)
  - Fortum (20 Mar 2026) forecast the Oulujoki spring flood at about 20% below average, with mid-March SWE of about 55 mm, well below normal — [Fortum release](https://mb.cision.com/Main/15253/4323943/3993580.pdf)
  - No damaging spring flood was found.
- **Ostrobothnia history (1984, 2000, 2006, 2011):** no event-specific € figures were found in this session.

**Exposure and modelled damage potential**
- **National 1-in-250 scenario:** a flood with a 1-in-250 return period nationally was estimated at about €550M of damage, roughly half to buildings. The figure dates from a 2000 study and was cited in a 2013 environment-administration report — [Raportteja 102/2013 (doria.fi), via search summary](https://www.doria.fi/bitstream/10024/93358/2/Raportteja_102_2013.pdf). Treat as dated.
- **Saimaa (lake system):**
  - Potential total damage across flood-mapped areas is almost €430M (national Silander & Parjanne 2012 method, excluding industry).
  - Building damage is €235.1M, of which more than 70% falls on holiday homes and cabins.
  - Traffic disruption is €142.7M.
  - Agricultural damage is €2.7–8M.
  - Source: [Kaakkois-Suomen ELY / SYKE, Raportteja 24/2023](https://vesi.fi/aineistopankki/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Raportteja_24_2023.pdf)
  - In the same report, direct industrial damage for one scenario set is €0.6M (more frequent flood), €2.7M (at or rarer than 1-in-100) and €20M (at or rarer than 1-in-250, 2020–2049 climate). It notes long floods can push industrial production-interruption losses "to hundreds of millions of euros".
- **Ivalo/Törmänen:** about 1,700 residents and about 650 residential buildings are in flood hazard in a very rare flood. Ivalo has been a significant flood-risk area since 2011 — [Paatsjoki preliminary assessment, 15 Mar 2024](https://www.vesi.fi/tulvariski-pdf/alustava-arviointi-vesistoalue-33817-71-2024617.pdf)
- **Highest risk concentrations nationally:** Pori (Kokemäenjoki) and Rovaniemi (Kemijoki) for river floods; Helsinki metro, Hamina–Kotka, Turku and Kemi for sea floods — [MMM/valtioneuvosto release 19 Dec 2024 (via search summary; page timed out)](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1410837/suomen-merkittavat-tulvariskialueet-on-nimetty)
- **Future risk:** SYKE estimates Finnish flood risk could double or triple by 2100, with large regional differences. Risk falls slightly in the short term (smaller snowpacks in the south) but rises in the long term. Finland's risk is small by international standards because lakes even out flows — [Verkkouutiset on SYKE report](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/suomen-tulvariskien-ennakoidaan-kasvavan-tulevaisuudessa/)
- **Snowpack trend:** in the Kemijoki basin, 1 April SWE showed an increasing trend over 1947–2001, with no or decreasing trend in southern Finland (Hyvärinen 2003, cited in [Helminen](https://sciencepolicy.colorado.edu/research_areas/sparc/research/projects/extreme_events/munich_workshop/helminen.pdf))

### Inferences
- Insured flood losses in Finland have historically been in the single-digit to low-double-digit €M range per event. A €10M parametric payout is therefore of the same order as the largest historical *total* building losses from a single spring flood, such as 2005 Lapland at €5–17M. Pricing must reflect that.
- The 2005 event (SWE about double normal, roughly 1-in-50 at Ounasjoki and Ivalojoki at the same time) is the canonical joint-exceedance case for a "≥2 gauges in a cluster" trigger in Lapland. 2018 and 2020 were near-miss years.
- The lack of industry flood-claims data since 2015 is a market pain point. Tulva's gauge-index data product could be pitched partly as a transparency fix.

### Gaps
- Insured and economic losses for 2018, 2020 and 2024 spring floods were not found.
- Ostrobothnia 1984, 2000, 2006 and 2011 loss figures were not found.
- No current (2024 assessment) national count of buildings or residents at risk across the 14 river areas was found. Area-level figures are in ELY preliminary assessment PDFs on vesi.fi, per river basin.
- No Pori/Kokemäenjoki € damage estimate for 1-in-100 or 1-in-250 was extracted. The 2015 summary plan lists risk objects but not € totals. The worst Pori scenario is an ice-jam flood whose return period "has not been reliably estimated".

## 5. Government role: ELY centres, Floods Directive, municipalities, uninsured losses

### Takeaway
Flood risk management follows the EU Floods Directive via the Act on Flood Risk Management (620/2010). MMM designates significant areas, ELY centres prepare maps and plans with basin flood groups, and SYKE/FMI's Tulvakeskus forecasts and certifies return periods. The state no longer compensates property flood damage. Infrastructure, municipal and some agricultural losses remain largely outside the private flood product.

### Cited Findings
- **Designation, 19 Dec 2024:** MMM designated 18 significant flood-risk areas for 2025–2030, 14 river (vesistö) and 4 coastal, based on ELY proposals from the preliminary assessment. Four former areas were dropped (Loviisa coast, Riihimäki centre, Lapua, Kemijärvi), credited to successful risk management and better mapping information. Flood groups were set up for each basin, with terms ending 22 Dec 2030 — [MMM/valtioneuvosto release (via search summary)](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1410837/suomen-merkittavat-tulvariskialueet-on-nimetty); [Lapin liitto minutes](https://lapinliittod10.oncloudos.com/kokous/2025147-9-34897.PDF)
- **Earlier rounds:** significant areas were designated in 2011 (21 areas, including Pori and Huittinen) and 2018 — [Varsinais-Suomi ELY, Kokemäenjoki FRMP summary 2015](https://cms.pori.fi/uploads/sites/2/2022/09/varsinais-suomen_ely-keskus_tiivistelma_kokemaenjoen_vesistoalueen_tulvariskien_hallintasuunnitelmasta_2015.pdf); [Pohjalaismaakunnat ELY](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70780902/pohjalaismaakuntien-alueelle-nimettiin-nelja-merkittavaa-tulvariskialuetta-pohjalaismaakunnat?lang=fi)
- **River areas identified so far (partial list of the 14):**
  - Ostrobothnia: Ilmajoki–Seinäjoki and Ylistaro–Koivulahti (Kyrönjoki), Laihia–Tuovila–Runsor (Laihianjoki), Lapväärtti (Lapväärtinjoki–Isojoki) — [Pohjalaismaakunnat ELY](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70780902/pohjalaismaakuntien-alueelle-nimettiin-nelja-merkittavaa-tulvariskialuetta-pohjalaismaakunnat?lang=fi)
  - Lapland: Ivalo (Paatsjoki), Rovaniemi and Kittilä (Kemijoki), Tornio (Tornionjoki). Five areas were named in Lapland in total, Tornio possibly counted as coastal/river — [vesi.fi Paatsjoki assessment](https://www.vesi.fi/tulvariski-pdf/alustava-arviointi-vesistoalue-33817-71-2025106-kuulemisen-jalkeen-2024.pdf); [Lapin liitto](https://lapinliittod10.oncloudos.com/kokous/2025147-9-34897.PDF)
  - North Ostrobothnia: Alavieska–Ylivieska (Kalajoki), lower Pyhäjoki, Pudasjärvi (Iijoki) — [Pyhäjärvi minutes](https://poytakirjat.pyhajarvi.fi/djulkaisu/kokous/2025297-16.PDF)
  - Pori (Kokemäenjoki) is listed among the largest-risk areas in the MMM release (via search summary).
  - Lapua was removed because new data showed fewer buildings at risk — [Pohjalaismaakunnat ELY](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70780902/pohjalaismaakuntien-alueelle-nimettiin-nelja-merkittavaa-tulvariskialuetta-pohjalaismaakunnat?lang=fi)
- **Flood mapping service:** run by Tulvakeskus. It covers hazard and risk maps for significant areas, hazard maps for about 100 other areas, and the entire coast — [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/)
- **Tulvakeskus:** a joint service of SYKE and FMI that forecasts, warns and maintains the national flood situation picture. It also issues return-period (exceptionality) statements to insurers — [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/)
- **Municipal role:** in significant areas, plans consider buildings in hazard zones, hard-to-evacuate sites, essential services, environment and cultural heritage. Land-use and building permits are a key measure (Kokemäenjoki plan) — [Kokemäenjoki FRMP summary](https://cms.pori.fi/uploads/sites/2/2022/09/varsinais-suomen_ely-keskus_tiivistelma_kokemaenjoen_vesistoalueen_tulvariskien_hallintasuunnitelmasta_2015.pdf). During the 2005 floods, Kittilä municipality bore significant material and labour costs for emergency protection — [Nordregio](https://archive.nordregio.org/en/Metameny/About-Nordregio/Journal-of-Nordregio/2008/Journal-of-Nordregio-no-1-1008/Flood-protection-in-Kittila-Finland/index.html)
- **Agriculture:** the state crop-damage scheme covered agricultural flood losses for 2014–2015 and moved to insurance from 2016. Finance Finland noted in 2013 that no crop-damage insurance products then existed — [HE 143/2013](https://www.edilex.fi/he/20130143); [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/vesitieto/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen/)

### Inferences
- Uninsured or residual losses include:
  - homes without home insurance, or with policies excluding flood;
  - floods below the 1-in-50 threshold;
  - municipal emergency protection costs;
  - roads, rail and utilities;
  - business interruption not covered by a property policy;
  - agricultural losses, where crop insurance take-up is uncertain.

  These are potential secondary buyers of a parametric index, such as municipalities, regional mutuals and ELY/state-linked entities.
- The 14 river areas form natural clusters. Lapland includes Ivalo, Kittilä, Rovaniemi and Tornio. Ostrobothnia includes the Kyrönjoki areas, Laihia and Lapväärtti. North Ostrobothnia includes Kalajoki, Pyhäjoki and Iijoki. Satakunta includes Pori. These match Tulva's "regional cluster" trigger design.
