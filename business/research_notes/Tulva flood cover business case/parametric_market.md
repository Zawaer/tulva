# Parametric insurance and catastrophe risk transfer market (flood, Europe/Nordics focus)

Research date: 8 October 2026. Every figure below carries its reference year and source. Several figures come from trade press (Artemis, Reinsurance News, Insurance Business) that reports primary data from Aon, AM Best, Swiss Re and others. Treat market-research-firm estimates (Mordor, GMI and similar) as low-reliability. Their definitions differ a lot.

## 1. Market size and growth: parametric insurance, cat bonds/ILS, flood share

### Takeaway
Estimates of the global parametric insurance market's size disagree by about 6x, depending on how "parametric" is defined: roughly $3.5bn to $21bn for 2025. Forecast growth clusters at 12–14% CAGR. The cat bond and ILS market is more reliably measured and hit records in 2025: about $61bn of cat bonds outstanding (Artemis) and $120–136bn of alternative reinsurance capital (Aon, Guy Carpenter/AM Best). Flood remains mostly uninsured, with about 80% of flood losses uninsured per Swiss Re Institute. I found no published figure for flood's share of the parametric or cat bond market.

## 2. Real examples of parametric/index flood covers, and Nordic cat risk transfer

### Takeaway
Live parametric flood products exist at three levels: property level (FloodFlash and Descartes on-site depth sensors), corporate river gauge (Swiss Re FLOW, which explicitly covers Nordic river gauges; Meteo Protect), and sovereign (CCRIF excess rainfall). Flood indemnity cat bonds (FEMA FloodSmart Re, Flood Re Vision 2039) show the pricing benchmark. I found no Nordic insurer-sponsored cat bond and no Finnish or Nordic regional parametric river-flood cover. That points to white space for Tulva, but it also means there is no local pricing precedent.

### Cited Findings
**Property-level sensor covers (UK/EU/US)**
- FloodFlash (UK) installs a connected depth sensor at the insured site. Clients pick up to three trigger depths with matching payouts. It paid a Storm Bert (Nov 2024) claim just over 10 hours after the trigger depth was reached. — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/floodflash-pays-parametric-uk-storm-bert-flood-claim-in-just-over-10-hours/); [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/floodflashs-parametric-coverage-ensures-rapid-payouts-to-uk-clients-after-floods/)
- FloodFlash settled a Storm Christoph (Jan 2021) claim in about 9h44m. Another source cites a record of 3h50m, so the sources conflict on the record time. — [StrategicRISK](https://www.strategic-risk-global.com/home/storm-christoph-claim-settled-within-ten-hours/1436262.article); [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/floodflashs-parametric-coverage-ensures-rapid-payouts-to-uk-clients-after-floods/)
- Descartes Underwriting's "Flood-at-Location" pays when water at the site exceeds a threshold measured by a connected sensor. Its US launch (June 2025) covers fluvial, pluvial and coastal flood with limits up to $70m, and a data-centre variant goes up to $80m. — [Insurance Edge, 25 Jun 2025](https://insurance-edge.net/2025/06/25/descartes-underwriting-launches-parametric-flood-solution-in-usa/); [Descartes](https://descartesunderwriting.com/white-papers/parametric-flood-insurance-data-centers-flood-location-us)

**River-gauge index covers (closest analogue to Tulva)**
- Swiss Re Corporate Solutions' FLOW is parametric water-level insurance for European companies exposed to high or low river levels. It references river gauges in Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, **the Nordic countries**, Portugal, Spain, Switzerland and the UK. It pays a fixed amount per day the index stays above or below an agreed level, and terms run up to three years. It was motivated by the 2018 low-water crisis on the Rhine. — [Swiss Re CS](https://corporatesolutions.swissre.com/insights/news/flow_new_parametric_water_level_insurance.html); [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/parametric-water-level-insurance-launched-in-europe-by-swiss-re/)
- Meteo Protect (France) offers parametric flood cover for river cruise operators and riverside businesses, triggered on river flood height. — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/meteo-protect-launches-parametric-flood-insurance-for-river-dependent-businesses/)
- A 2024 overview of parametric flood structures covers depth sensors, river gauges, satellite flood extent, and modelled-loss triggers. — [The Insurer, Dec 2024](https://theinsurer.com/parametric-insurer/news/defining-deluge-a-guide-to-parametric-structures-for-flood-risk-2024-12-04)
- Swiss Re (March 2026) says parametric flood solutions that use multi-source data, including advanced sensors, will be key to success. — [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/multi-source-data-key-to-parametric-flood-insurance-success-swiss-re/)

### Inferences
- Swiss Re FLOW is the most direct competitor or analogue: same gauge-index logic, already covering Nordic gauges, fixed payouts. - Flood Re Vision 2039 is the closest European pricing comparator: about a 2.8% attachment probability, close to Tulva's 1-in-50 (2%) trigger, priced at about 3.4x expected loss (5.75 / 1.7).

## 3. ILWs, index triggers in reinsurance, and pricing practice (is a 35% load realistic?)

### Takeaway
Market practice prices cat layers at **multiples of expected loss**, not at EL + 35%. Cat bonds averaged 3.71x EL in 2024, 3.0x in 2025, and about 2.4–2.5x in 2026 year-to-date, even in a very soft market. Flood-specific deals priced at about 3–3.4x EL. A 1-in-50 layer with a low expected loss sits at the high-multiple end. Tulva's 1.35x multiple is therefore about half to a third of market pricing. It would likely not attract risk capital without subsidy, unless Tulva is framed as "technical pure premium" before expenses and cost of capital.

### Cited Findings
**Loss multiples (cat bond spread ÷ expected loss)**
- AM Best analysis of Artemis data (25 Sep 2026): the weighted average multiple was 3.71x for 2024, 3.0x for 2025 (H1 2025 3.28x, H2 2025 2.40x), 2.5x for H1 2026 and 2.4x for 2026 year-to-date. In H1 2026 the weighted average spread fell 96bp while weighted EL rose 30bp versus H1 2025. AM Best urges investors to be more selective. — [Artemis, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.artemis.bm/news/as-cat-bond-loss-multiples-tighten-am-best-urges-investors-towards-greater-selectivity/)
- Deal-level examples: Finca Re 2025-1 (industry-loss trigger) priced at about 2.84x EL, against 4.23x for Finca Re 2022-1 (EL 1.82%, spread 7.75%). Mona Lisa Re 2025-1 A priced at 800bp on a 4.14% EL, or 1.9x. — [Artemis Finca Re](https://www.artemis.bm/news/canopius-secures-125m-finca-re-cat-bond-renewal-priced-at-low-end-of-guidance); [Insurance Insider ILS](https://insuranceinsiderils.com/article/2e4a3ccp0pjgp4jnicav4/all-topics/catastrophe-bonds/repeat-cat-bond-sponsors-continue-to-secure-lower-spreads-in-december-deals)
- Flood cat bonds: Flood Re Vision 2039 had an EL of 1.7% and guidance of 5.0–5.75%, about 2.9–3.4x EL. FloodSmart Re 2023-1 A had an EL of 5.35% and guidance of 16.25–17.25%, about 3.0–3.2x. — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/flood-re-secures-40-upsized-140m-vision-2039-cat-bond-at-top-end-pricing/); [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/deal-directory/floodsmart-re-ltd-series-2023-1/)
- Academic work (Variance journal): the multiple falls as EL rises. Low-EL (remote) layers carry higher multiples, high-EL layers lower ones. — [Variance, "An Analysis of the Market Price of Cat Bonds"](https://variancejournal.org/article/120790-an-analysis-of-the-market-price-of-cat-bonds)

**ILWs and index triggers**
- European windstorm ILWs and cat bonds commonly reference the **PERILS** industry-loss index. PERILS publishes an index within six weeks of an event, with updates at three, six and twelve months. — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/perils-launches-european-windstorm-industry-loss-index/); [Insurance Insider](https://www.insuranceinsider.com/article/2876fnf4db62x2qpp1lsx/ilws-first-to-use-perils-trigger)
- European wind ILW indicative rates historically rose 10%+ at the €5bn and €10bn industry-loss triggers after northern European storms (undated, historical). — [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/ilw-pricing-rises-for-european-wind-and-storm-related-perils/); [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/ilw-pricing-rises-after-renewals-for-us-eu-jp-coverage-triggers/)
- US ILW rates fell about 10–20% at 1 January 2026 (Howden Re). — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/ilw-rates-fall-10-20-at-jan-renewals-growth-to-normalise-in-2026-howden-re/)
- Industry-loss-trigger cat bonds (for example Finca Re) are a mainstream index-trigger structure for primary insurers and reinsurers. — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/canopius-secures-125m-finca-re-cat-bond-renewal-priced-at-low-end-of-guidance)

### Inferences
- **The 35% load is too low.** Tulva prices at 1.35x EL. Market evidence for comparable remote layers is about 2.4–3.7x for cat bonds (2024–26) and about 3–3.4x for flood specifically. That implies a risk load of about 140–270% of EL, not 35%. A defensible hackathon assumption is a 2.5–4x EL multiple, with 35% presented as a stress or "subsidised/public-private" case.
- Cost-of-capital check (illustrative, my own calculation). A €10M binary limit with a 2% annual trigger probability has an EL of €0.2M. A fully collateralised writer ties up about €10M. At even a 6–8% excess return requirement, the capital cost is about €0.6–0.8M a year. The premium then needs to be about €0.8–1.0M, or 4–5x EL. At 1.35x the premium is €0.27M, which does not cover the capital cost. A diversified reinsurer holding less than full collateral would need less, which is why market multiples sit at about 2.5–3.5x rather than 5x.
- Binary payouts, where the full €10M pays on trigger, have EL equal to trigger probability times limit. Remote, binary, single-peril and uncertain-model risk (a joint ≥2-gauge exceedance estimated from a short gauge history) all justify loads at or above market average, not below.
- Expect model/parameter uncertainty to be priced explicitly. A 1-in-50 event estimated from roughly 50–100 years of gauge data has wide confidence intervals.
- Typical primary-insurer use of index covers: industry-loss or parametric layers sit alongside indemnity cat XL programmes as top-up or aggregate protection, buying speed and avoiding loss-adjustment delay in exchange for basis risk.

### Gaps
- I found no published rate-on-line for a 1-in-50 European ILW. Broker ILW pricing sheets (Howden Re, Gallagher Re, Marsh Re) are subscription-only.
- The final clearing spread for Vision 2039 was not confirmed (guidance top end 5.75%).

## 4. Basis risk: definition, management, regulatory/accounting treatment, buyer objections

### Takeaway
Basis risk is the mismatch between the index payout and the actual loss. It is the main buyer objection to parametric cover and the main obstacle to recognition as risk mitigation in regulatory capital. Mitigations include denser or multi-source triggers, multiple trigger levels, and combined triggers. Under Solvency II's standard formula, recognising non-indemnity cover is restricted where basis risk is material; firms may need internal-model approval. Under IFRS 17, an index contract counts as insurance only if it transfers significant insurance risk that adversely affects the holder.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: the trigger does not match the actual loss, so the payout may be more or less than the loss. Descartes and PwC also discuss mitigations such as multiple trigger levels, finer-resolution data and combined indices. — [Descartes Underwriting](https://descartesunderwriting.com/insights/what-basis-risk-parametric-insurance-practical-explanation); [PwC Switzerland 2024 (PDF)](https://www.pwc.ch/en/publications/2024/Basis_risk_in_parametric_insurance_challenges_and_mitigation_strategies.pdf)
- Academic framing (2025): basis risk is better measured as the distance between payment and true loss than as correlation. High correlation only shows linear dependence, not closeness of compensation. The paper proposes expectile-based payout schemes that are optimal for basis risk. — [arXiv 2505.02607](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02607)
- FloodFlash's design (up to three depth triggers with stepped payouts) is a practical way to reduce basis risk compared with a single binary trigger. — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/floodflash-uk-floods-hit-parametric-triggers-for-some-policies/)
- In UK floods, only *some* FloodFlash policies hit their triggers, which shows basis risk in practice: affected clients below the trigger depth received nothing. — [Artemis](https://www.artemis.bm/news/floodflash-uk-floods-hit-parametric-triggers-for-some-policies/)
- Solvency II: under the standard formula, risk-mitigation recognition is governed by Delegated Regulation Arts. 208–214. CEIOPS consultation responses (2009) state that where the recovery trigger differs from the reinsured's loss, there is no obvious way to handle it in the standard SCR, so the basis risk allowed must be restricted. Material basis risk pushes firms toward (partial) internal-model approval. — [CEIOPS comments template CP 52/09](https://register.eiopa.europa.eu/CEIOPS-Archive/Documents/Advices/CEIOPS-SEC-115-09-Comments-and-Resolutions-Template-on-CEIOPS-CP-52-09.pdf); [DNB Q&A on reinsurance risk mitigation](https://www.dnb.nl/media/fr3lzst0/q-a-on-recognition-of-risk-mitigation-techniques-using-reinsurance-contracts-in-the-solvency-ii-standard-formula.pdf); [EIOPA opinion on risk mitigation techniques](https://www.dirittobancario.it/sites/default/files/allegati/eiopa_-_opinion_risk_mitigation_techniques.pdf)
- IFRS 17: an insurance contract transfers significant insurance risk and compensates when the insured event *adversely affects the policyholder*. Risk from a variable not specific to a party can be financial risk, and substance prevails over form. A pure index contract with no link to holder loss may therefore be classified as a derivative (IFRS 9) rather than reinsurance. — [BDO](https://bdo.nz/en-nz/blogs/financial-reporting-insights/for-the-purposes-of-ifrs-17,-what-is-an-insurance-contract); [Deloitte](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/ch/Documents/audit/deloitte-IFRS-17-for-non-insurers-new.pdf)
- A 2026 academic review covers how trigger indices for index-based flood insurance are built and how basis risk is assessed. — [MDPI Water 18:01274 (2026)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/water/water-18-01274/article_deploy/water-18-01274.pdf)

### Inferences
- Tulva's "≥2 gauges in a cluster" rule is a sensible way to reduce false triggers (a single gauge malfunction or a local ice jam). But it raises the chance of a miss when the loss is concentrated near one gauge. Quantify both error types (false positive and false negative) against historical insured flood losses, for example from Finanssiala / Finance Finland or the cedant's own claims.
- For a Finnish cedant to get Solvency II capital relief, Tulva should show high correlation between the index and the cedant's regional flood loss portfolio. Alternatively, add a light loss-warranty condition (a cedant loss ≥ €X), which turns it into a dual-trigger and helps the IFRS 17 "adversely affects" test. Dual triggers are the standard ILW approach.
- A stepped payout (for example 50% at 2 gauges, 100% at 3+, or scaled by the exceedance level) would reduce basis risk compared with a binary €10M.

### Gaps
- I found no EIOPA or FIN-FSA (Finanssivalvonta) guidance specific to parametric reinsurance under current Solvency II (post-2025 review).
- I found no quantified survey of customer objections. The basis-risk objection is well documented qualitatively but not measured.

## 5. Seasonal forecasts, pre-season information and adverse selection; forecast-based financing

### Takeaway
If buyers can observe snowpack or seasonal forecasts before buying, they will buy more in high-risk years (intertemporal adverse selection). This is well documented for crop insurance sales closing dates. Standard defences are fixed purchase deadlines before the informative season, multi-year terms, and forecast-conditional pricing. Forecast-based financing (Red Cross FbF, Start Network) uses forecasts as the *trigger* rather than an input to selection. That is a design alternative: a pre-emptive payout once snowpack or forecast discharge crosses a threshold.

### Cited Findings
- Crop insurance study (1994): if farmers can forecast yields before the sales closing date, they can choose which years to buy, which raises programme losses. The authors built early-season weather models to predict low corn yields before the purchase deadline. — [Review of Agricultural Economics, via IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/revage/v16y1994i3p441-451..html)
- A weather-derivative dissertation (University of Bonn) shows buyers may base purchases on prior-month weather, so product structure and contract start timing must be set against this. — [Bonn dissertation](https://bonndoc.ulb.uni-bonn.de/xmlui/handle/20.500.11811/5836)
- Adverse selection in agricultural insurance is tackled by making cover mandatory or by incentives that widen the buyer pool. — [Arbol, Weather Insurance 101 Part III](https://www.arbol.io/post/weather-insurance-101-part-iii)
- Commercial parametric products are sold in season-specific windows. Aviva's GCS parametric product, for example, covers specific seasonal or weather events for two weeks to five months. — [Aviva Canada](https://www.aviva.ca/en/business/find-insurance/products/gcs-parametric/)
- Swiss Re and WTW launched a forecast-triggered policy (Oct 2025) that activates automatically when the UK Met Office or Met Éireann issues a red weather warning. — [CIR Magazine, Oct 2025](https://www.cirmagazine.com/cir/c2025100201.php); [Insurance Journal, 2 Oct 2025](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2025/10/02/842165.htm)
- Red Cross FbF releases funds according to forecast thresholds set in Early Action Protocols. In Bangladesh (July/August 2017 floods) the protocol activated and paid unconditional cash ahead of the flood. — [Red Cross Climate Centre / PrepareCenter FbF PDF](https://preparecenter.org/wp-content/sites/default/files/forecast_based_financing.pdf)
- FbF trigger design accounts for past flood magnitude and willingness to "act in vain", which is the forecast-error tradeoff, the FbF equivalent of basis risk. Example: GloFAS-based triggers in Uganda. — [Coughlan de Perez et al., HESS 2016](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/20/3549/2016/); [NHESS 2015](https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/15/895/2015/nhess-15-895-2015-relations.html)
- IFRC's Forecast-based Action fund uses combined triggers such as rainfall forecast plus rising river levels. — [Eco-Business / Reuters](https://www.eco-business.com/news/red-cross-plans-to-fight-disasters-with-fast-funding/)
- Start Network's Start Ready pairs impact-based forecasts with pre-arranged funding. In Pakistan (August 2025), riverine-flood forecast triggers released money ahead of peak flooding. — [Start Network Start Ready evaluation 2026 (PDF)](https://startnetwork.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/START%20READY%20Evaluation%202026.pdf); [Start Network blog](https://startnetwork.org/learn-change/news-and-blogs/crisis-anticipation-how-working-together-understand-risk-will-give-us-confidence-act)
- Guidance note: well-designed triggers support decision-making for anticipatory action. — [PreventionWeb](https://preventionweb.net//node/71508)

### Inferences
- **Finnish spring floods are highly predictable months ahead.** Snow water equivalent from SYKE (the Finnish Environment Institute) and the hydrological forecasts on vesi.fi are informative from about February to March. If Tulva sells after snowpack is observed, buyers will select against it in big-snow years. Options:
  1. A fixed **purchase deadline** before snow accumulation is informative (for example 1 November or 1 December), with an annual term covering the next spring.
  2. **Multi-year contracts** (2–3 years, as Swiss Re FLOW offers), which remove year-by-year selection.
  3. **Snowpack-conditional pricing**: quote premium as a function of the 1 March SWE anomaly, so late buyers pay a forecast-adjusted price.
  4. An **anticipatory tranche**, FbF-style: a partial early payout (for example 20–30%) when forecast peak discharge or SWE exceeds a threshold, with the remainder on observed gauge exceedance.
- Because the buyers are insurers, who will run their own snowpack models, the adverse-selection risk is higher than for retail buyers. Selling with a pre-season deadline, or pricing on forecasts, is essential for credibility with reinsurers or ILS investors.
