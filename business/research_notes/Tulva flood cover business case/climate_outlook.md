# Climate outlook for Finnish spring (snowmelt) floods and flood risk: stable, shrinking or shifting?

Researched 8 Oct 2026. Every number carries the date or period of its source. Where I could not verify something, it is listed under Gaps rather than stated.

## 1. SYKE/FMI projections: snow water equivalent (SWE) and spring flood peaks to 2050/2100, south vs north

### Takeaway
Every assessment in the SYKE line of work (Veijalainen et al. 2010, then the SYKE/FMI 2026 update using CMIP6 models) finds the same pattern. Snowmelt-driven spring floods shrink and arrive earlier, while autumn and winter floods grow. The decline is strongest in the south and centre, including the coastal (Ostrobothnian) rivers, and around 65–66°N. In Lapland, spring floods stay roughly at today's size until about 2050 and then decline, although some scenarios still produce large spring floods late in the century. Lake-outflow rivers in the south (Kymijoki, and Kokemäenjoki/Pori) are the exception: floods there grow, driven by winter rain and lake volume rather than snow.

### Cited Findings
- **Veijalainen et al. 2010 (J. Hydrology 391:333–350; SYKE + Univ. Turku; 67 sites, 20 climate scenarios, WSFS model, periods 2010–2039 and 2070–2099 vs 1971–2000):** On average, 100-year floods in Finland decreased by 8–22% by 2070–2099. Spring (March–June) 100-year floods decreased by 15–40% on average, while floods in other seasons increased by 12–40%. — [Veijalainen et al. 2010, author copy](https://en.vedur.is/media/ces/Journal_of_Hydrology_Veijalainen_etal.pdf); [UTU record](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/3665792?lang=en_US)
- Same study: in northern Finland, more than 95% of annual maximum floods were caused by spring snowmelt in the reference period (1971–2000). Most coastal rivers can flood from either snowmelt or heavy rain. — [Veijalainen et al. 2010](https://en.vedur.is/media/ces/Journal_of_Hydrology_Veijalainen_etal.pdf)
- Same study: "In northern Finland the snowmelt floods on average remained unchanged in 2010–2039", because increased snowfall offset warming. Some northern scenarios still produced large spring floods in 2070–2099. In the north, temperature change mattered most: cool scenarios gave the largest floods and warm scenarios (HadCM3) the smallest. — [Veijalainen et al. 2010](https://en.vedur.is/media/ces/Journal_of_Hydrology_Veijalainen_etal.pdf)
- Same study: the largest decreases in 100-year floods were "around latitudes 65–66° north", and the decreases got smaller both further south and further north. The more a site's floods came from spring melt in the reference period, the more its floods decreased (correlation −0.70 / −0.73). — [Veijalainen et al. 2010](https://en.vedur.is/media/ces/Journal_of_Hydrology_Veijalainen_etal.pdf)
- Same study: in southern Finland, autumn and winter floods increased considerably and became the largest floods of the year by 2070–2099. Floods in the large central lakes and their outflow rivers increased. — [Veijalainen et al. 2010](https://en.vedur.is/media/ces/Journal_of_Hydrology_Veijalainen_etal.pdf)
- **Site results relevant to Tulva (2070–2099):**
  - **Lapua (Lapuanjoki, Ostrobothnia):** 100-year discharge −11% to −32%. 100-year inundation area −12% to −90%. Water level as much as 1.38 m lower in the minimum scenario.
  - **Pori (Kokemäenjoki):** inundation area increased in both the min and max scenarios (+31.3% in the max scenario). Water levels were higher than the reference in all scenarios, by up to 31–36 cm. The authors say Pori needs more attention to flood protection.
  - **Kittilä (Ounasjoki):** inundation area +7% to −18%. Water level +0.1 m to −1.04 m. The authors describe this as "most probably also close to the reference period".
  - Source: [Veijalainen et al. 2010](https://en.vedur.is/media/ces/Journal_of_Hydrology_Veijalainen_etal.pdf)
- Same study cites the 2010-era expectation that warming of 2–6 °C would cut snow accumulation by 40–70% by the end of the century (Vehviläinen & Huttunen), and that annual precipitation would rise by 13–26% by the 2080s (Ruosteenoja & Jylhä 2007). — [Veijalainen et al. 2010](https://en.vedur.is/media/ces/Journal_of_Hydrology_Veijalainen_etal.pdf)
- **Ilmasto-opas (FMI/SYKE climate guide), "Lumi vähenee":**
  - Southern Finland, 2070–2099 vs 1961–1990, A2 scenario: snow-cover days fall to just under half, and snowpack water content (SWE) falls 80–90%.
  - Northern Finland, same period and scenario: snow days fall 20–30% and SWE falls 40–70%, partly offset by more winter precipitation.
  - The page gives no publication date and no 2050 figures.
  - Source: [Ilmasto-opas, Lumi vähenee](https://ilmasto-opas.fi/artikkelit/lumi-vahenee/)
- **Lapland, nearer term:** maximum SWE falls by almost 10% by about 2040 under all four scenarios, and the snow season shortens by 2–3 weeks. This comes from a regional presentation dated 2026, which I saw only as a search snippet and did not open. — [Tunturi-Lapin kehitys, Ilmastonmuutoksen vaikutukset Lapissa (2026)](https://tunturilapinkehitys.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/ilmastonmuutoksen-vaikutukset-lapissarovaniemi20260504.pdf)
- **SYKE Report 30/2026, Veijalainen (SYKE), Virman and Mäkelä (FMI), "Ilmastonmuutos ja virtaamien muuttuminen Kemi-, Kymi- ja Lieksanjoen alueilla – vuoden 2026 arvio"** (CMIP6, SSP1-2.6/2-4.5/5-8.5, WSFS-P model, reference 1991–2020; commissioned by Kemijoki Oy). Its key findings:
  - SWE decreases clearly in all scenarios. The decline is strongest in the Kymijoki basin (south).
  - Under SSP1-2.6, snow stops declining much after 2040–2069. Under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, strong decline continues to 2100.
  - The authors note their 9-model subset runs slightly warm, so it may slightly overestimate snow loss.
  - "Spring floods caused by snowmelt decrease, particularly from the mid-century onward, while winter floods increase."
  - Annual mean discharge changes are only −2% to +9% in 2040–2069, but seasonal shifts are large (winter discharges up, summer down).
  - Source: [SYKE raportteja 30/2026 (PDF, hosted by Kemijoki Oy, Aug 2026)](https://www.kemijoki.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/sykera2026-30veijalainenkemijoki.pdf)
- **Same 2026 report, Kemijoki (Rovaniemi/Kittilä basin):**
  - In 2020–2049, "Kemijoki floods may still remain at current magnitude and large spring floods are still common". Floods mainly decrease from 2040–2069 onward because there is less snow.
  - The timing of floods moves from late May/early June to May, or even April, by late century.
  - Some of the 10 scenarios keep floods at roughly today's size. Autumn and early-winter (Nov–Dec) floods increase in every scenario.
  - The authors warn that better treatment of extremes might raise future flood estimates: "large floods must still be prepared for".
  - Source: [SYKE 30/2026](https://www.kemijoki.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/sykera2026-30veijalainenkemijoki.pdf)
- **Same 2026 report, Kymijoki (Lower Kymijoki, SE):**
  - High flows already occur throughout Nov–Apr, and spring melt floods are not the largest.
  - Winter floods grow clearly, and average flood size grows under climate change. Growth is large in the wettest scenarios in 2070–2099.
  - Simulated Kymijoki winter discharge is +9.5% (2020–2049), +10.8% (2040–2069) and +13.2% (2070–2099). These are vs 1991–2020 and come from Table 10, whose column alignment was garbled in the text extraction, so check them against the PDF.
  - Source: [SYKE 30/2026](https://www.kemijoki.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/sykera2026-30veijalainenkemijoki.pdf)
- **Same 2026 report, caveat:** its flood analysis was "preliminary and rough". It should be used only for small and medium floods, not for very rare large floods, which "may behave differently" (citing Jakkila et al. 2025). — [SYKE 30/2026](https://www.kemijoki.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/sykera2026-30veijalainenkemijoki.pdf)
- A SYKE view reported by Kaleva: warming increases winter floods but reduces spring floods in southern and central Finland, while in northern Lapland spring floods still grow for now and decline only later. I saw this only in a search snippet; the article is undated in the results. — [Kaleva, "Talvitulvat lisääntyvät ja kevättulvat vähenevät Etelä-Suomessa"](https://www.kaleva.fi/talvitulvat-lisaantyvat-ja-kevattulvat-vahenevat-e/2317569)
- **Government / EU Floods Directive, 3rd cycle (designation of 19 Dec 2024):**
  - The Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (MMM) designated 18 significant flood-risk areas (14 river, 4 coastal). Pori (Kokemäenjoki) and Rovaniemi (Kemijoki) carry the largest risks.
  - Four previous areas were dropped: Loviisa coast, central Riihimäki, Lapua and Kemijärvi. MMM attributed this partly to successful risk management and better mapping.
  - Climate change "increases flood risks in many river basins and along the coast", but no entirely new risk areas were identified. MMM flags floods in all seasons and fast-developing situations as forecasting challenges.
  - Source: [Valtioneuvosto press release 19.12.2024](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1410837/suomen-merkittavat-tulvariskialueet-on-nimetty)

### Inferences
- **Ostrobothnia cluster (6 areas), the coastal rivers near 62–64.5°N:** this sits in or close to the latitude band with the largest projected decreases. Snowmelt-flood risk is the most likely to shrink here, and the 2024 de-designation of Lapua fits that, although MMM credited risk management. Expect a slow downward drift in spring-peak severity, with more winter and rain-driven events.
- **North/Lapland cluster:** risk is roughly stationary to about 2050, with continued potential for large spring floods (SYKE 2026). After 2050 it declines and shifts earlier, but with wide scenario spread. This cluster is the most durable market for a snowpack-conditioned product.
- **Satakunta–SE cluster:** this cluster is not mainly a snowmelt risk going forward. Pori (Kokemäenjoki) and Kymijoki are projected to see stable or increasing flood risk, from winter floods, frazil ice and sea level at Pori. A March SWE conditioning variable will explain less and less of the loss variance here, so a winter-flood or ice peril component is needed.
- **Overall answer to "stable, shrinking or shifting":** the risk is shifting in season (spring to winter) and in geography (snowmelt risk shrinks fastest in the south and west), rather than simply shrinking. Joint (co-occurrence) risk across clusters probably falls, because southern and northern peaks become less synchronous.

### Gaps
- I found no explicit SYKE numbers for 2050 SWE or for spring-peak change for the Ostrobothnian rivers (Lapuanjoki, Kyrönjoki, Pyhäjoki, Kalajoki) beyond Veijalainen 2010's Lapua result. Veijalainen's 2012 dissertation and the 2018/2021 SYKE assessments (Parjanne et al. 2021) were not retrieved.
- The SYKE 2026 report's per-scenario flood percentages for Kemijoki are shown only in figures (Figs 16–19) and were not extractable as numbers.
- No Tornionjoki or Ivalojoki-specific climate projections were found.

## 2. Observed trends, ice-jam and frazil risk, recent extreme events

### Takeaway
The observed record already shows the projected direction. Since 1911, high-flow events have come earlier and got smaller, most clearly on the coast and less so in the north, while winter flows have risen. Ice and frazil risk is mixed: frazil floods are a current problem on Kymijoki and Kokemäenjoki and are emerging on Kemijoki, while historical ice-jam severity on some southern rivers has fallen. The largest recent Lapland spring floods I could verify were in 2018 and 2020, driven by record snow plus rapid warm spells. I found no formal attribution study for them. I could not verify a major 2024 Lapland flood event.

### Cited Findings
- **Lintunen et al. 2024, J. Hydrology: Regional Studies (36 unregulated stations, 1911–2021):** high-flow events show "advancing timings and decreasing magnitudes, notably in the coastal region and less so in the north". At 21 stations they now occur 6 to 68 days earlier. Monthly volumes changed at 30 stations, while annual totals stayed relatively unchanged. — [UTU research portal, "Changes in the discharge regime of Finnish rivers"](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/387506879?lang=en_US); [UTUPub full text](https://www.utupub.fi/bitstreams/915b28de-164b-4907-abd1-ff8bddd266bf/download)
- **Korhonen & Kuusisto 2010** (trends to 2004): an increase in winter runoff and earlier spring peaks, "but very few significant changes in annual flood magnitudes". This is cited in [Veijalainen et al. 2010](https://en.vedur.is/media/ces/Journal_of_Hydrology_Veijalainen_etal.pdf). **Korhonen 2019** (Univ. Helsinki dissertation): ice cover duration on Finnish inland waters has shortened, and winter and spring discharges have increased. — [PEER news on Korhonen 2019 dissertation](https://www.peer.eu/news/detail/doctoral-dissertation-the-duration-of-the-ice-cover-of-finnish-inland-waters-has-shortened-and-seasonal-winter-and-spring-discharges-have-increased); also cited in [SYKE 30/2026](https://www.kemijoki.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/sykera2026-30veijalainenkemijoki.pdf)
- **Frazil ice (hyyde), SYKE 30/2026:**
  - Climate change likely increases frazil-flood risk (citing Aaltonen et al. 2010).
  - Kymijoki: frazil formation is "very common" and occurred very often in recent years. The risk continues in the near term but may decline late in the century, as hard frosts become rare.
  - Kemijoki: frazil and difficult ice conditions used to be rare but have become more common in recent years. Rising winter flows and later freeze-up will likely increase frazil and ice-jam risk.
  - High ice-season flows can break the ice cover and raise ice-jam risk (Uusikivi & Vehviläinen 2017). This becomes clearly more likely in all scenarios.
  - Source: [SYKE 30/2026](https://www.kemijoki.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/sykera2026-30veijalainenkemijoki.pdf)
- **Kokemäenjoki (Pori/Huittinen):** a 2010 IAHR study notes the middle Kokemäenjoki is "known for harmful frazil ice floods". It projects that winter discharges increasing under climate change (2010–2039 and 2040–2069 vs 1971–2000) may change frazil and anchor-ice formation and frazil jams. — [IAHR library record](https://www.iahr.org/library/info?pid=19477)
- **Ice-jam history:** Tornionjoki has a 300+ year ice-breakup record and multiple ice-jam floods with monetary losses in the last century. The Aura River (SW Finland) shows "fewer severe ice jam events in the twentieth century". — [Norrgård & Helama, The Cryosphere preprint tc-2021-326](https://tc.copernicus.org/preprints/tc-2021-326/tc-2021-326-manuscript-version4.pdf); [Åbo Akademi, Aura river ice jams](https://research.abo.fi/en/publications/an-ice-breakup-as-in-the-good-old-days-ice-jams-in-the-aura-river/)
- **2018 Lapland flood (May 2018):** forecast to be the worst spring flood in decades. It was expected to close roads around Kittilä and possibly require limited evacuations, with a record flood forecast for Rovaniemi (previous large floods 1993 and 1997). The cause cited was "rapid temperature swings and record snow amounts in Lapland this winter". — [Barents Observer, May 2018](https://thebarentsobserver.com/en/ecology/2018/05/lapland-braces-worst-spring-floods-decades)
- **2020 Lapland flood:**
  - The Lapland ELY Centre forecast more than 1,000 m³/s at Kittilä (similar to the 2005 flood) and 4,000–4,200 m³/s at Rovaniemi.
  - The Kemijoki flood was "unusually large" and exceeded the damage limit in Rovaniemi. Preparation kept damages minor.
  - Barents Observer described winter 2019–20 as the "snowiest winter in years".
  - Sources: [ELY Lapland 2020](https://ely-keskus.fi/uutiset-2020/-/asset_publisher/OvA3wfzIQ4AO/content/flood-water-will-rise-to-the-damage-limit-in-rovaniemi-and-kittila-in-the-next-few-days-lapland-); [ELY Lapland, floods over](https://www.ely-keskus.fi/sv/-/floods-in-lapland-are-over-and-water-levels-are-falling-lapland-); [Barents Observer 2020](https://thebarentsobserver.com/en/ecology/2020/05/barents-region-prepares-flooding-after-snowiest-winter-years)
- **Recent quiet years:**
  - 2025: the Lapland authorities' flood meeting of 13 May 2025 showed low forecast flood peaks. — [ELY Lapland meeting 5/2025](https://www.ely-keskus.fi/documents/d/ely-keskus/kokouskooste_tulvakokous_lappi_5_2025_13052025)
  - 2026, Lapland: on 16 Apr 2026, snow had melted earlier than usual and was well below average except in the Ounas, Muonio and Ivalo basins (near average). Peaks were expected in May and below average. The Rovaniemi (Kemijoki) peak was "possibly the smallest in the observation history". — [Lapin elinvoimakeskus, flood meeting 1/2026 (16.4.2026)](https://elinvoimakeskus.fi/documents/d/elinvoimakeskus/kokouskooste_tulvakokous_lappi_1_16042026-pdf)
  - 2026, Oulujoki: on 20 Mar 2026, Fortum forecast the spring flood about 20% below average, with basin SWE about 55 mm in mid-March, after a low-snow winter. I saw this only in a search snippet. — [Fortum release via Cision (20.3.2026)](https://mb.cision.com/Main/15253/4323943/3993580.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Volatility, not just the mean, is what matters for pricing.** In Lapland, 2018 and 2020 (record snow) were followed by 2025–2026 (low peaks, a possible record-low Rovaniemi peak). The year-to-year driver is snowpack, which supports Tulva's conditioning on March SWE.
- For Pori, Huittinen and Lower Kymijoki, the frazil and winter-flood peril is significant and is not captured by March SWE.

### Gaps
- **The "2024 Lapland floods":** I found no reliable source for a major 2024 Lapland spring flood. The 2024 items found were planning documents, such as updated Rovaniemi flood maps. Verify this premise before using it in pitch material.
- I found no event attribution study (e.g. World Weather Attribution or FMI) for the 2018 or 2020 Lapland floods.
- No damage totals for 2018 or 2020 were found.
- No quantified projection of ice-jam frequency exists for Tornionjoki or Kokemäenjoki.
- I found no peer-reviewed cloudburst (summer convective) flood projection specific to the 14 river areas. SYKE 2026 says heavy-rain changes affect mainly summer floods and matter most in small catchments; in large rivers, summer floods are generally not the annual maximum.

## 3. Forecasting skill: WSFS, March lead time, vesi.fi

### Takeaway
SYKE's WSFS covers all of Finland and assimilates satellite snow cover and SWE. Its short-range forecasts are reliable for 3–5 days, longer for large rivers and lakes. Each spring, ensemble seasonal outlooks give peak size and timing as probabilities weeks ahead: by mid-March to mid-April, authorities already publish "below/near average" peak outlooks with probabilities. I found no published skill scores (e.g. hit rate of March outlooks for spring peaks).

### Cited Findings
- WSFS is an HBV-based model that covers the whole of Finland, including cross-border basins (about 390,000 km²). It is used for flood forecasts and warnings, and assimilates satellite snow-cover and SWE data. — [SYKE, Watershed Simulation and Forecasting System](https://www.syke.fi/en/services/modeling/water-and-marine-modelling/watershed-simulation-and-forecasting-system); [Climateguide.fi article](https://climateguide.fi/articles/finnish-environment-institutes-watershed-simulation-and-forecasting-system)
- SYKE told WMO (Dec 2023) that forecasts are "reliable up to 3–5 days, large rivers and lakes even longer", and that the limiting factor is the weather forecast. — [WMO, Operational flood forecasting and warnings (Dec 2023)](https://public.wmo.int/sites/default/files/2023-12/Operational_flood_forecasting_and_warnings_141223.pdf)
- WSFS-P is a process-based successor (EGU 2024; Kolhinen et al. 2025) and is the model used in the SYKE 2026 projections. — [EGU24-15445](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU24/EGU24-15445.html); [SYKE 30/2026](https://www.kemijoki.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/sykera2026-30veijalainenkemijoki.pdf)
- Example of a probabilistic seasonal outlook, 16 Apr 2026, about 4 weeks before peaks:
  - "Forecasts show large spread in timing and magnitude in all rivers."
  - Muonionjoki: about a 20–30% probability that the flood reaches average size.
  - Ivalojoki, Ounasjoki, Tornionjoki and Teno: peaks around mid-May, below average.
  - Source: [Lapin elinvoimakeskus 16.4.2026](https://elinvoimakeskus.fi/documents/d/elinvoimakeskus/kokouskooste_tulvakokous_lappi_1_16042026-pdf)
- An example of a March-issued outlook: on 20 Mar 2026, Fortum published an Oulujoki spring-flood outlook (about −20% vs average) based on mid-March SWE (snippet only). — [Fortum/Cision 20.3.2026](https://mb.cision.com/Main/15253/4323943/3993580.pdf)
- MMM (Dec 2024) notes that floods in all seasons and fast-developing situations make forecasting harder. — [Valtioneuvosto 19.12.2024](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1410837/suomen-merkittavat-tulvariskialueet-on-nimetty)

### Inferences
- A March SWE-conditioned outlook is technically mainstream in Finland: operators and authorities already issue March–April peak outlooks. Tulva's edge would be translating these outlooks into a priced, joint (cross-area) loss distribution, not the forecast itself. Remaining spring weather (April–May rain and the speed of warming) adds irreducible spread, as the 2018 rapid-warming case shows.
- Rain-on-snow and winter floods are less predictable at a March horizon, and they are the growing share in the south. So the value of conditioning on March SWE is highest in Lapland and declining in the south and west.

### Gaps
- No quantitative verification of WSFS seasonal (March-issued) spring-peak forecasts was found, such as CRPS, a correlation of March forecasts against observed peaks, or a hit rate.
- The vesi.fi forecast product's spec (ensemble size, lead time) was not retrieved.

## 4. Insurance implications and damage potential in significant flood-risk areas

### Takeaway
Finnish flood cover is mostly embedded in home and property insurance. Insurers define an "exceptional" flood as a probability of 1/50 years or rarer. The hazard is non-stationary and shifting by season and region, which argues for annual re-pricing and a separate winter, rain and frazil peril, especially for Satakunta–SE. I found no national figures for residents at risk in, or damage potential of, the 2024 significant flood-risk areas. Only local figures and qualitative MMM statements were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Market structure:** almost all insurers include flood cover in home and property policies, which now also covers heavy rain and sea-level rise. Responsibility for exceptional-flood compensation moved from the state to insurers. Insurers treat a water rise as exceptional at a 1/50-year probability or rarer, and terms vary by company. The article was undated in the search results. — [Verkkouutiset, "Tulvavahinkojen korvaaminen nopeutuu"](https://www.verkkouutiset.fi/a/tulvavahinkojen-korvaaminen-nopeutuu-12997/)
- **Context for scale only:** Finance Finland statistics cited by MTV give more than 22,000 household water-damage claims per year and more than €90M paid per year. This covers all water damage (pipes, appliances), not floods specifically, and the year is not stated in the snippet. — [MTV Uutiset](https://www.mtvuutiset.fi/artikkeli/viisi-pahinta-kosteusvaurion-aiheuttajaa-varo-naita/3327400)
- **Designations (19 Dec 2024):** 18 significant areas (14 river, 4 coastal). Pori and Rovaniemi carry the largest risks. Lapua and Kemijärvi were dropped (Kemijärvi after the Pöyliöjärvi dam was raised). — [Valtioneuvosto](https://valtioneuvosto.fi/-/1410837/suomen-merkittavat-tulvariskialueet-on-nimetty)
- **Kemijoki 2024 preliminary assessment (15.3.2024):** proposes Rovaniemi city and the Kittilä urban area as significant areas for 2024–2030, based on past floods, dense settlement in hazard areas, places that are hard to evacuate, and transport links. Rovaniemi's flood map was updated and new maps were drawn for Isohaara–Pelkosenniemi in 2024. — [vesi.fi, Kemijoki preliminary assessment 2024](https://www.vesi.fi/tulvariski-pdf/alustava-arviointi-vesistoalue-33325-65-2025106-kuulemisen-jalkeen-2024.pdf)
- **Local resident counts from the 2024 preliminary assessments** (search snippets of vesi.fi PDFs):
  - Pyhäjoki: about 400 residents in the 1/100-year flood area, of whom about 320 are behind flood protection.
  - Lestijoki (Himanka): about 260.
  - Perhonjoki: about 110 in the very-rare flood area.
  - Jalasjärvi: about 60.
  - Source: [vesi.fi preliminary assessment PDFs (2024)](https://www.vesi.fi/tulvariski-pdf/alustava-arviointi-vesistoalue-50-2024320.pdf)
- **Pori adaptation signal:** Pori's modelled 100-year water levels in 2070–2099 were higher than the reference in all scenarios, by up to 31–36 cm. — [Veijalainen et al. 2010](https://en.vedur.is/media/ces/Journal_of_Hydrology_Veijalainen_etal.pdf)
- **Climate Change Panel, reported by Yle (article more than 4 years old):** Pori has the greatest flood risk in Finland and Rovaniemi the second. — [Yle](https://yle.fi/a/3-12111938)

### Inferences
- **Non-stationarity:**
  - Historical return periods from 1971–2000 or 1991–2020 data will tend to overstate spring-melt risk in Ostrobothnia, and understate winter, frazil and lake-outflow risk in Pori and Kymijoki.
  - Pricing should use a trend-adjusted or recent-window baseline, re-estimated annually.
  - Large scenario spread argues for explicitly capturing parameter uncertainty in premiums.
- **Value of the snowpack-conditioned outlook:** conditioning on March SWE yields large year-to-year swings in expected loss in Lapland (high-snow 2018/2020 vs low-snow 2026). This creates a real underwriting and capital-allocation signal, strongest in the North cluster. As the climate warms, more southern losses will occur outside the spring window, so the product should either be scoped to spring and Lapland, or add a winter-flood module.
- **Business framing:** the core Lapland spring-flood market looks stable to about 2050 (SYKE 2026). Ostrobothnia snowmelt risk is a shrinking line. Pori and Kymijoki are a growing but differently driven line.
- **Moral hazard and regulation:** de-designations (Lapua, Kemijärvi) show that risk also changes through mitigation such as embankments, dams and regulation. Pricing should track ELY mitigation projects as well as climate.

### Gaps
- National totals were not found for residents living in 1/100 or 1/250 flood zones of the 14 river areas, for damage potential (€) in the 2018 or 2024 designations, or for annual flood-claims totals from Finanssiala. MMM and SYKE flood-risk management plans for 2022–2027 and the 2024 PFRA summary report (SYKE) likely contain these, but were not retrieved.
- The official 2024 list naming all 14 river areas was not in the press-release text. Confirm against MMM's decision annex.
- The criterion of 500–1,000 permanent residents for significance comes from a 2010 memo cited in municipal documents. It is unverified here.
