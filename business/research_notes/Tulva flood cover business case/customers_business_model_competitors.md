# Tulva: Customers, Business Model Options and Competitors (Finland/Nordics, as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: research budget limited to ~15 searches/fetches. Many commercial figures (license prices, commissions, Finnish insurer cat-programme sizes) are not publicly disclosed; these are flagged in Gaps rather than estimated as fact. Items under "Inferences" are reasoning, not sourced fact.

## Q1. Potential customers and pain points: who has budget and a reason to buy?

### Takeaway
Since 1 Jan 2014 Finnish river-flood losses moved from the state to private home/property insurance, so Finnish primary insurers (led by OP Pohjola, LocalTapiola, If) now carry correlated spring-flood accumulation risk and are the natural first buyers; reinsurers are the capacity providers/partners rather than first customers. Public-sector and hydropower buyers are plausible but weaker on budget/mandate.

### Cited Findings
- Finland ended state compensation of flood damage on 1.1.2014 and moved to an insurance-based system; the Act on compensation for damage caused by exceptional floods (284/1983) was repealed. State aid can still be granted for private roads after exceptional floods. — [Taloustaito: Rankkasateet voivat voimistua – korvaako vakuutus](https://www.taloustaito.fi/koti/rankkasateet-voivat-voimistua--korvaako-vakuutus/); background legislative proposal [HE 295/2010](https://www.edilex.fi/he/20100295)
- Almost all Finnish home insurances now include flood cover (river floods, sea-level rise, heavy rain), but river-flood cover often requires a high threshold; some narrower products do not compensate even 1-in-50-year floods. — [Taloustaito](https://www.taloustaito.fi/koti/rankkasateet-voivat-voimistua--korvaako-vakuutus/)
- OP: property and home insurance may cover flood damage when water rises "exceptionally", defined as a level occurring once in 50 years or less frequently. — [OP: Tulvat ja vakuutusten korvattavuus](https://www.op.fi/en/-/tulvat-ja-vakuutusten-korvattavuus)
  - Relevance: Tulva's 1-in-50 gauge trigger mirrors the existing Finnish policy-wording threshold, so the index aligns with how insurers' claims actually arise.
- Finnish non-life premiums written grew 3% to €5.6bn in 2024. — [Finance Finland, Finnish Insurance in 2024](https://www.finanssiala.fi/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/finnish-insurance-in-2024.pdf)
- Market concentration: in 2020 OP had >30% of Finnish non-life GWP, LocalTapiola ~26.7%; in 2022 the ranking was Pohjola (OP), LocalTapiola, then If P&C's Finnish branch (Statista, based on Finance Finland data). — [Statista via search excerpt](https://www-statista-com.ezproxy.canberra.edu.au/statistics/1087022/market-share-of-non-life-insurance-companies-in-finland-by-gross-premiums-written); FIN-FSA publishes company-level stats at [Finanssivalvonta non-life statistics](https://betatest.finanssivalvonta.fi/en/statistics/insurance/non-life-insurance/)
- Finland's official flood maps (SYKE) cover >100 areas including 18 designated significant flood-risk areas; damaging floods can also occur outside mapped areas. — [vesi.fi: Am I living in a flood risk area?](https://www.vesi.fi/en/water-topics/am-i-living-in-a-flood-risk-area/)
  - Note: our team's "14 river flood-risk areas" should be reconciled with the official 18 (the official count includes coastal/stormwater areas depending on the cycle).
- 2018 Lapland spring flood: forecast worst in ~20 years; Kittilä ELY centre predicted levels up to 70 cm above the damage threshold; Tornio and Kittilä expected extensive damage; Copernicus EMS activation EMSR284. — [RCI/Eye on the Arctic, May 2018](https://www.rcinet.ca/eye-on-the-arctic/2018/05/14/finland-lapland-saimaa-flooding-weather/); [Copernicus EMSR284](https://emergency.copernicus.eu/EMSR284)
- Historic friction: in an earlier Kittilä flood, insurers said homeowners were not eligible under home insurance, and homeowners asked the government for help (pre-2014 regime). — [Yle](https://yle.fi/a/3-5216703)
- Finland is among European countries where less than half the population has flood insurance (European study, cited via Climate Change Post). — [Climate Change Post](https://www.climatechangepost.com/sweden/insurance-and-business)
- Public-sector precedent for parametric index cover in Europe: Italian Episcopal Conference cover (Generali, Swiss Re, Floodbase data) with flood-index trigger, €180m limit over 3 years, >25,000 churches; described by Swiss Re as the largest parametric cover in Europe. — [Swiss Re case study](https://www.swissre.com/our-business/public-sector-solutions/case-studies/protecting-religious-cultural-heritage.html)
- Municipal parametric flood: Descartes' public-sector offering and a US sensor-based municipal flood case study (e.g. $10m location limit, $6m payout at 3.9 ft depth example); no European municipal Descartes client found. — [Descartes public sector](https://descartesunderwriting.com/industries/public-sector); [Descartes municipal case study](https://descartesunderwriting.com/case-studies/building-resilience-us-municipalities-against-flood-risk-sensor-based-parametric)
- NATURANCE (CMCC-led, 2024–2026) is designing a product covering water boards' costs when deliberately flooding upstream rural land to protect cities; pilot in Emilia-Romagna is a next step (design phase only). — [LSE Grantham](https://www.lse.ac.uk/granthaminstitute/?p=87821)

### Inferences
- Primary insurers (OP Pohjola, LocalTapiola, If, Fennia, Turva) have the clearest reason to buy: they hold post-2014 flood exposure, the 1-in-50 threshold in their wordings creates correlated claim clusters exactly when multiple rivers exceed 1-in-50, and Solvency II nat-cat capital/ORSA work needs a defensible view of joint risk. Pain points: (a) vendor models treat Finland inside a pan-European rainfall-driven model (see Q3), (b) spring flood is snowmelt-driven and forecastable from March snowpack, which generic models may not exploit, (c) small market means vendor models are low priority for calibration.
- OP and LocalTapiola together hold >55% of Finnish non-life GWP (2020 figures), so two customers define the domestic market; If (Nordic, Sampo group) is the gateway to Sweden/Norway.
- Reinsurers (Swiss Re, Munich Re, Hannover Re, SCOR, Gen Re) are better framed as capacity partners/data licensees for the parametric product than as first SaaS customers; they already have in-house flood models (Swiss Re owns Fathom).
- Municipalities (Kittilä, Pori, Seinäjoki, Inari) bear uninsured infrastructure/emergency costs but have small budgets and procurement rules; the state stopped compensating homes but still subsidises private roads, so the state remains a de facto backstop and may resist paying premiums. Weak near-term buyers; better as pilot partners or via ELY/SYKE research projects.
- Hydropower (Kemijoki Oy, Fortum) benefits from high spring inflows (revenue upside) and faces dam safety/regulation costs; their need is more for inflow forecasting than loss transfer. Banks with mortgage exposure need climate stress-test data (ECB climate risk expectations) – a data-licensing rather than insurance buyer. These are inferences; not verified with sources here.

### Gaps
- No public figure found for Finnish insured flood losses (2018 Lapland or any year) from Finance Finland.
- 2024 company-level market shares for Fennia and Turva not found.
- No sourced information found on Kemijoki/Fortum, MTK, or banks' flood-risk purchasing.
- No source found on whether Finnish insurers already buy parametric or flood-specific reinsurance.

## Q2. Business model options, precedents and revenue; which is most realistic

### Takeaway
Comparable parametric players (Descartes, FloodFlash) started as MGAs/coverholders backed by reinsurer capacity and later grew or were acquired; but MGA licensing and capacity are slow to secure. For a hackathon-stage team the realistic path is analytics/index licensing to Finnish insurers and reinsurers first, with the parametric cover structured via a reinsurer or established MGA partner.

### Cited Findings
- Descartes Underwriting: founded 2018 as an MGA for parametric weather/nat-cat risks for corporates; established full-stack insurer Descartes Insurance in 2022; growing ~30%/yr, targeting $500m premium in the medium term; parametric to remain the majority of business. — [The Insurer interview, Oct 2025](https://theinsurer.com/ti/interview/descartes-targets-500-million-premium-with-30-annual-growth-as-ai-and-rapid-2025-10-13); [Reuters via TradingView](https://tr.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L6N3T80CS:0-descartes-looks-beyond-parametric-to-diversification-opportunities-where-it-has-data-advantage)
- Descartes launched US Flood-at-Location parametric cover (June 2025), limits up to $70m, surplus lines in all 50 states. — [Descartes newsroom](https://descartesunderwriting.com/newsroom/descartes-expands-us-flood-offer-highly-responsive-parametric-flood-location-product)
- FloodFlash (UK sensor-based parametric flood MGA and Lloyd's coverholder) agreed to be acquired by NormanMax in Feb 2025; FCA approval by April 2025; continues as MGA/coverholder. Price undisclosed; FloodFlash had raised >$20m from investors (a "$20M acquisition" headline appears to conflate funding with price). — [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/normanmax-to-acquire-floodflash/); [Insurance Business](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/breaking-news/floodflash-joins-normanmax-in-major-parametric-deal-526277.aspx); [Dealroom](https://app.beta.dealroom.co/news/feed/normanmax-acquires-floodflash-for-20m)
- Retail parametric MGAs have closed, highlighting challenges of retail parametric. — [The Insurer: MGA closures highlight challenges of retail parametric](https://theinsurer.com/?p=40402) (headline only reviewed)
- General MGA economics: base commission typically ~10–25% of GWP depending on line and expense load, plus possible profit commission tied to loss ratio (general guidance, not parametric-specific). — [Beancount MGA bookkeeping guide, 2026](https://beancount.io/fr/blog/2026/07/18/managing-general-agent-mga-bookkeeping-contingent-profit-commission-guide)
- Cat model license pricing (only, dated, data point): AIR's terrorism model was reportedly priced ~$200,000 for large property insurers and up to $50,000 for smaller companies (early 2000s). Smaller insurers often have a reinsurer or broker model their book instead of licensing. — [Risk.net](https://risk.net/node/1503527); [Insurance Forums thread](https://www.insurance-forums.com/community/threads/catastrophe-modelling-in-p-c-insurance.62562/) (anecdotal)
- Data/model licensing precedent: Floodbase supplied flood data to the Generali/Swiss Re Italian church parametric cover. — [Swiss Re case study](https://www.swissre.com/our-business/public-sector-solutions/case-studies/protecting-religious-cultural-heritage.html)
- Reinsurer acquiring flood analytics: Swiss Re bought Fathom (Dec 2023, undisclosed price), keeping the brand and working with Swiss Re Reinsurance Solutions to distribute flood data, maps and models. — [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/swiss-re-acquires-flood-risk-modeller-fathom/)

### Inferences
- Unit economics illustration (not sourced, arithmetic only): a €10M-limit index cover at a 1-in-50-ish joint trigger has expected loss ~€0.2M/yr if the joint probability is ~2%; with typical cat loading the premium might be ~2–4x EL (€0.4–0.8M). At a 10–25% MGA commission that is only ~€40–200k revenue per contract. So MGA revenue only becomes meaningful with many contracts/Nordic scale.
- SaaS/analytics license: if large-insurer cat model peril licenses historically ran in the tens to hundreds of thousands USD/yr (dated AIR data point), a niche Finland spring-flood module could plausibly charge €30–100k/yr per insurer. With ~5 Finnish insurers + a handful of reinsurers/brokers, domestic ceiling is low single-digit €M ARR; Nordic expansion (Sweden/Norway snowmelt rivers) needed.
- Most realistic sequence: (1) paid pilot/consulting with one insurer (OP or LocalTapiola) or a reinsurer's Nordic team; (2) license the index and joint-risk engine; (3) partner with a reinsurer (or an existing parametric MGA like Descartes) to issue the cover, taking a structuring/data fee rather than becoming a regulated MGA early. Becoming an MGA needs FIN-FSA authorisation (insurance distribution) and capacity – inference, not verified here.
- Public-sector advisory (ELY/SYKE/municipalities) is viable as grant-funded R&D revenue, not a scalable core.

### Gaps
- No public commission rates for parametric coverholders specifically; no current RMS/Verisk/JBA price lists.
- Descartes/FloodFlash revenue and commission income not disclosed.

## Q3. Competitors, their offerings and Finland coverage (incl. snowmelt)

### Takeaway
Finland is covered by the big vendors: Verisk's Inland Flood Model for Central Europe explicitly lists Finland, Sweden, Norway (and Baltics), and Moody's RMS Europe Inland Flood HD states it models snowmelt flooding (country list not confirmed). Tulva's differentiation therefore cannot be "nobody covers Finland" but rather "snowpack-conditioned seasonal joint risk across Finnish gauges + ready-to-use parametric index", which generic precipitation-driven pan-European models are not built for.

### Cited Findings
- Verisk (AIR) Inland Flood Model for Central Europe covers Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Israel, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Monaco, Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Sweden, Switzerland, Turkey, UK; the documentation page does not mention snowmelt. — [Verisk Touchstone 11.0 docs: Inland flood zones](https://docs.risksolutions.verisk.com/Touchstone/11.0/ts-tsre_all/help_ts_loss-analysis_flood-zones.html)
- Verisk describes the model as fully stochastic, event-based, for precipitation-induced flooding on- and off-floodplain with rainfall-runoff and hydraulic river modeling; the 2019 expansion announced detailed river networks for Austria, Czech Republic, Germany, Poland, Switzerland. — [Verisk press release](https://verisk.com/newsroom/air-worldwide-releases-expanded-inland-flood-model-for-central-europe); [Verisk model landing page](https://integration.riskmodeling.verisk.com/models/landing-pages/AIR-Inland-Flood-Model-for-Central-Europe)
- Moody's RMS Europe Inland Flood HD models: datasheet (via search excerpt) says 18 river basins, 8,289 catchments, 15 countries, and all inland flood sources incl. fluvial, pluvial, snowmelt and groundwater; earlier versions covered 13/14 countries. Country list (and Finland inclusion) not confirmed — the datasheet URL returned 404. — [RMS datasheet (search excerpt)](https://forms2.rms.com/rs/729-DJX-565/images/rms-european-inland-flood-datasheet.pdf); [RMS Europe flood whitepaper](https://rms.com/offer/europe-flood-whitepaper)
- JBA Risk Management: global flood maps for every country at 30m, 5m for UK, Ireland, USA, continental Europe and major populated Australia; river, surface water and coastal. No Finland-specific product page found. — [JBA Flood Maps](https://www.jbarisk.com/products/flood-maps/); [JBA Consulting](https://www.jbaconsulting.com/2023/10/05/delivering-global-flood-mapping-solutions/)
- Fathom: acquired by Swiss Re (Dec 2023); global flood maps/models; historically limited by terrain data (SRTM) in data-poor areas. — [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/swiss-re-acquires-flood-risk-modeller-fathom/); [The Stack](https://thestack.technology/swiss-re-buys-fathom-flood-data-specialist-and-the-stacks-award-winner)
- Descartes: parametric MGA/insurer with public-sector and flood products (US flood-at-location up to $70m). — [Descartes](https://descartesunderwriting.com/newsroom/descartes-expands-us-flood-offer-highly-responsive-parametric-flood-location-product)
- FloodFlash: sensor-triggered parametric flood MGA, now owned by NormanMax (2025). — [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/normanmax-to-acquire-floodflash/)
- Floodbase: flood index/data provider used in Generali's Italian parametric programme. — [Swiss Re case study](https://www.swissre.com/our-business/public-sector-solutions/case-studies/protecting-religious-cultural-heritage.html)
- Nordic local player: SCALGO offers DynamicFlood hydrodynamic modelling with technical sessions for Denmark, Finland, Sweden, Norway (planning/municipal flood mapping, not insurance pricing). — [SCALGO webinar](https://scalgo.com/en-GB/webinars/dynamicflood-technical-session)
- Public data: SYKE maintains Finland's flood map service (vesi.fi / flood centre). — [vesi.fi](https://www.vesi.fi/en/water-topics/am-i-living-in-a-flood-risk-area/)

### Inferences
- Because Verisk covers Finland in a precipitation-driven framework, a sales pitch should demonstrate (with backtests: 2005, 2013, 2018 Lapland) that snowpack-conditioned joint exceedance probabilities differ materially from vendor-model output for Finnish portfolios. RMS claims snowmelt modelling, so Tulva must show added value via seasonal conditioning (March forecast) and Finnish gauge-level calibration rather than snowmelt per se.
- SYKE/FMI are more likely data suppliers/partners (open hydrological and snow data) than competitors; they don't sell risk transfer.
- Reinsurers with in-house models (Swiss Re/Fathom) could be competitors for the index but also the most likely capacity partner.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether Moody's RMS Europe Inland Flood HD includes Finland, nor whether Verisk explicitly models snowmelt for Finland.
- KatRisk, CatInsight, Vaisala, FMI commercial services: not researched due to budget; KatRisk Europe coverage of Finland unknown.
- No pricing for any competitor.

## Q4. Go-to-market for insurtech in Finland: programmes, funding, sales cycle

### Takeaway
Business Finland's Tempo (€60k grant) is closed and replaced by a "Sprint" pilot for small companies (2026); R2B grants (80% funding, typical €400–800k projects, €25M 2026 budget) suit a university-spun team; EIC Accelerator offers up to €2.5M grant plus €1–10M equity.

### Cited Findings
- Tempo: max €60,000 grant, 75% of costs, max €80,000 project; last call closed 3 Oct 2025 and will not reopen; replaced by "Sprint funding" pilot for small companies with rapid international growth potential, launching early 2026 (amounts not found). — [Business Finland Tempo](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/services/funding/funding-services/tempo-funding/); [Business Finland 2026 funding update](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/whats-new/news/2026/what-do-business-finlands-funding-services-look-like-at-the-beginning-of-2026/)
- Research to Business (R2B): call 1/2026 (1 Dec 2025–4 Mar 2026) 80% grant, typical projects €500–800k; call 2/2026 (8 Jun–16 Sep 2026) 80% grant, typical €400–700k, decisions targeted Jan 2027; €25M total across 2026 calls. Requires a research organisation as applicant. — [R2B call 1/2026](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/services/funding/calls/2026/research-to-business--funding-call-12026/); [R2B call 2/2026](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/services/funding/calls/2026/r2b-funding-call-2-2026/)
- Business Finland reports record demand for R&D funding in 2026. — [Business Finland news](https://www.businessfinland.fi/en/whats-new/news/2026/demand-for-rd-funding-reaches-record-levels/)
- EIC Accelerator 2026: grant up to €2.5M (70% of costs, lump sum, up to TRL 8); equity €1M (new 2026 floor)–€10M via EIC Fund; short proposals continuously, with batch cut-offs (e.g. 2 Sep and 4 Nov 2026 reported); one source claims €15M equity cap (unconfirmed). June 2026 round selected 38 companies. — [madri+d EIC fact sheet](https://www.madrimasd.org/blogs/demadridaeuropa/files/2025/11/Ficha-resumen-EIC-Accelerator.pdf); [Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/startup-accelerators-applications-august-2026/); [ERA Austria](https://community.era.gv.at/news-items/eic-selects-38-start-ups-and-smes-for-funding-under-latest-round-of-eic-accelerator/)

### Inferences
- Practical GTM: approach OP (largest non-life writer) and LocalTapiola cat/actuarial and reinsurance-buying teams ahead of the 1 January renewal cycle (spring flood index would be priced in autumn/early winter and conditioned on March snowpack) — reinsurance timing is inference.
- Insurer sales cycles for cat analytics are typically long (often cited as 6–18 months with model validation); not sourced here.
- An R2B project with a Finnish university (e.g. Aalto/Helsinki) + SYKE/FMI data is the best-fit non-dilutive route for a hackathon team.

### Gaps
- OP Lab / LocalTapiola innovation programmes: not verified in this research (status in 2026 unknown).
- Sprint funding amounts unknown.
- No sourced insurer sales-cycle benchmark.

## Q5. Market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM)

### Takeaway
Global cat modeling software/analytics is roughly a $2–3bn market (2025) growing ~9–12%/yr per market-research firms of limited methodological transparency; the Finnish slice is tiny, so SOM must be framed in terms of a handful of Finnish/Nordic insurers plus parametric premium.

### Cited Findings
- Catastrophe modeling market: $2.10bn (2025) to $5.59bn (2034), 11.5% CAGR (software and analytics revenues). — [TrendX Insights](https://trendxinsights.com/syndicated-market-research-reports/catastrophe-modeling-market/)
- Catastrophe exposure management software: $2.1bn (2025) to $4.5bn (2034), 9.2% CAGR. — [Marketintelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/catastrophe-exposure-management-software-market)
- Cat modeling with satellite data: ~$2.8bn base 2025, $6.9bn by 2034 at 12.4% CAGR. — [Marketintelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/catastrophe-modeling-with-satellite-data-market)
- Finnish non-life premiums written: €5.6bn (2024). — [Finance Finland](https://www.finanssiala.fi/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/finnish-insurance-in-2024.pdf)
- Swiss Re: insurers covered ~40% of nat cat economic losses in 2023; ≥$12bn insured nat cat losses in 2023 from floods. — [Insurance Business on Swiss Re/Fathom](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/reinsurance/news/breaking-news/swiss-re-announces-acquisition-of-fathom-470221.aspx)
- Descartes targets $500m premium (scale of a leading parametric player). — [The Insurer](https://theinsurer.com/ti/interview/descartes-targets-500-million-premium-with-30-annual-growth-as-ai-and-rapid-2025-10-13)

### Inferences
- TAM (analytics): global cat modeling ~$2–3bn. SAM: Nordic flood analytics — perhaps low tens of €M (Nordics are a small share of global cat spend); SOM year 3: e.g. 3–6 license customers × €30–100k = €0.1–0.6M ARR. Purely illustrative.
- TAM (risk transfer): Nordic flood reinsurance/parametric premium – unknown; bottom-up by insurer property GWP × flood share × ceded share is the recommended method.

### Gaps
- No public figure for Nordic/Finnish flood reinsurance premium or cat-programme sizes; no recent Nordic renewal data found (latest Nordic renewal source found was dated, ~2011: [Commercial Risk](https://www.commercialriskonline.com/stability-in-nordic-region-as-high-capacity-cancels-out-losses/)).
- Market-research figures are from low-transparency publishers; treat as indicative.
