**One timing story, everywhere:** the cover is sold in autumn, before the snow falls. In March the snow update
tells the insurer how much joint risk it holds. The price is set in autumn, not after the snow is known.

> Lapland's snow had warned us two months EARLIER. [pause] Tulva turns that warning into a PRICE.

## 2 · The gap (0:13–0:34)

> Finland's rivers share ONE snowpack, so they flood TOGETHER. [pause] If rivers flooded one by one, a spring
> with many floods would be rare. In reality, it happens about FOUR times as often. [pause] Insurers carry that
> joint risk, and they buy their cover in autumn, BEFORE the snow falls.

## 3 · The product (0:34–0:54)

> The Tulva Index pays when SEVERAL northern rivers pass a RARE flood level in the same spring. [pause] Priced as
> if rivers were independent, it would be about FIVE times too cheap. [pause] Flood models start from RAIN.
> Tulva starts from SNOW, and prices rivers TOGETHER.
