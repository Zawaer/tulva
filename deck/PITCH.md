words, it fits inside the video). Everything else: 284 live words at 140 wpm ≈ 2:02 + the 37 s video ≈ 2:38.
At most one spoken number per slide. Spoken lines are the `>` lines; `build_deck.py` copies them into each slide's

## 1 · Hook (0:00–0:09)

> In May 2005, Kittilä and Ivalo flooded together. The March snow had warned us.
> Nobody used that warning. Tulva does.

## 2 · The gap (0:09–0:26)

> Finnish rivers share one snowpack, so they flood together. Many river areas flood in the same spring about four
> times as often as independence predicts. That joint risk sits on insurers' books, and covers are bought before
> the snow is known.

## 3 · The product (0:26–0:57)

> The Tulva Index pays when several northern gauges pass a rare flood level in one spring, with the limit sized
> to the insurer's own retained joint loss. Priced jointly: five to seven percent of the limit a year. Sold in
> autumn; in March, the snow update shows how much joint risk the insurer holds.
> Vendor flood models are rainfall-driven, SYKE's outlook goes unpriced, FLOW covers one gauge. Tulva prices the
> joint, snow-driven risk.

## 4 · Demo video (0:57–1:34), narrated live

The video is silent; say this over it, live (≈70 words, ≈30 s, starts with the map):

> This is our live app. Lines join river areas that flood together. Now I set the March snow outlook to high,
> and the northern cover's price jumps. Here are real shots from IQM's Emerald chip: after a snowy March, joint
> floods turn up far more often. Next, the whole flood model on one quantum chip, matching the joint tail that
> independence misses. Finally, spring 2005: Lapland lights up together.

## 5 · Quantum, in practice (1:34–1:49)

> The whole model loads onto one IQM chip and reproduces the joint tail. On IBM and IQM, our calibrated
> amplitude estimates land within twenty to thirty percent of exact, and the snow signal survives.

## 6 · Quantum, honestly (1:49–2:04)

> Honestly: classical Monte Carlo still wins today. Today's chips handle about fifty two-qubit gates; rarer
> events drown in noise. The method is ready, the hardware isn't yet, so now is the time to build the pipeline.

## 7 · Business (2:04–2:25)

> Finland is our pilot: it has the best open data. We license the engine to insurers, then reinsurers and
> brokers across Nordic and Central European flood. Why now? From January 2027, Solvency II
> charges capital for Finnish flood. First step: a paid pilot this autumn, ahead of the January renewal.

## 8 · Close (2:25–2:38)

> Everything you saw was built this weekend: data pipeline, joint model, pricing, and runs on three quantum
> chips. Tulva prices spring floods as a joint risk, from this winter's snow.
